# Fresh-eyes audit before sending: model, results and page

Date: 2026-11-19, on main at `7a3165d` (after the cut-cost change, PR #57). Four independent reviewers with no prior context read
the cost side, the demand side, the page (as four kinds of reader) and the literature on what drives shoppers' choices
(`what_drives_choice.md`). Each recomputed the figures it quotes. I re-ran the claims marked **checked**. Nothing in the model is
changed by this note; section 6 is the plan.

## 1. Verdict

- **The qualitative answer survives every check**: cultivated meat stays a niche at the low-lactate example, and a tenth of the market needs cells that make almost no ammonia or lactate and shoppers who come to like it about as much as meat. That it stands after the fixes is very likely (a rough judgement made while building the model with an AI assistant; not measured and not reviewed by an expert): every fix found moves the cost by dollars, not by the factor the conclusion would need.
- **The numbers attached to it are not yet ready to send.** Three things would be caught by a careful reader on the first page:
  headline costs that sit outside or at the bottom of their own ranges; an all-favourable example whose 30% share is mostly a demand assumption
  the page does not show; and stale text from three versions of the model.
- **Five model defects change the headline**, each small or moderate, in both directions (section 3). After fixing them I expect
  the low-lactate example's cost near $30–35 a kilo (median) and its share at 0.2–0.5% of world meat (a rough judgement made while building the model with an AI assistant; not measured and not reviewed by an expert). Adopted on 2026-11-19 and 2026-11-20 with later batches, the fixes took it to $49.50 and 0.047%.

## 2. Answers to the questions asked

**"The all-favourable example is $10, with $10.7–18.4. Does that make sense?"** The arithmetic is right and the presentation is wrong. The $10
puts every estimate at its most likely value. In that case almost every estimate can only hurt: ammonia tolerance at its favourable
end leaves the cost at $9.96 and at its unfavourable end gives $17.06; the least medium flow, $9.96 and $16.71; filter life, $9.21
and $19.03. So 3% of draws come in below the point and the median is $13.5 (**checked**). The same holds, less sharply, for every
row: the low-lactate example's point ($26.4) is the 28th percentile of its draws (median $29.9). Meanwhile the share column shows a median. So the
table pairs a cost and a share that do not belong to each other. Fix: the headline is the median with its range; the point is a
labelled second line ("$10.0 if every estimate lands on its most likely value; 3 draws in 100 do better").

**"Is share at equal price all? What do people consider? Is this the best method?"**
- What people weigh, by strength of evidence: taste and buying again after trying; price (real but not sufficient: in the main
  experiment beef keeps a majority even with the alternatives at half price); naturalness, disgust and trust (the first barrier
  for cultivated meat, stated evidence only); familiarity and availability; the name (small); animal welfare and environment (weak
  for mainstream shoppers); demographics (weak); country (large). There are still no usable sales data for cultivated meat.
- The dial is a recognised summary (Slade 2018 and Van Loo 2020 report exactly it) and the model is already a mixed logit with one
  random taste. One dial is adequate **if documented as a judgement**. A never-buy group already falls out of the spread of
  liking: at half the price of meat cultivated takes 22%, at a fifth 28%, nearly free 32%, so about 68% never buy (**checked**
  for half price). That is stronger resistance than the surveys show, so no new class is needed; the page should print it.
- What is wrong is around the dial, not the dial: its uncertainty range (finding D1), the all-favourable example's value (D2), the tier rule
  (D3) and the cut (D4).

**"Should the short version show the market-share plot?"** Yes. The one chart for the first screen: long-run share of world meat
against the cost of a kilo of cells, with two lines (13% and 55% at equal price), the cases as markers with their cost ranges,
a line at price parity and a line at plant-based meat's share today. It shows at a glance that share stays under 1% until cost
falls below about $20, and that below it shoppers' liking matters as much as cost.

**"Several scenarios in the detailed view, or just explain the one selected?"** Explain the one selected. Today five different
pickers set the same state, and the case names change between views (the same case had a short name in one view and a
long one in another). One page, one case selector, three named cases plus "your own settings"; the ladder
becomes one chart; the forecast, Humbird and the company's report move to "other estimates".

**"Blue underlined links."** The page has no general link style, so links outside four special blocks fall back to the browser's
default blue and purple. A thin underline in the page's accent colour fixes it and stays accessible (section 5).

## 4. Errors and imprecision in the text

**Definitions.** "Share" has four meanings: of all four choices or of meat buyers (13.0% is 14.5% of meat buyers); "world meat"
includes seafood (24% of the total and 45% of the low-lactate example's sales: the low-lactate example is 0.38% of meat proper and 1.0% of seafood); by product
weight a fifth of a cut is plant; and with every product at equal price the world share is 12.5%, not 13%. Sections 3–5 of RESULTS
run on the reported and reference costs; there is no by-type table for the low-lactate example or the all-favourable example.

**Names.** "Reference case", "floor" and "Humbird's optimistic design" are one case; "cautious" means three things; "default" means
The low-lactate example at the top of the page, the company's $34 in the methods and Humbird's $12.8 in the vessels text. The frontier ($7.16) is
below "the all-favourable example" ($10.0) and the text does not say why. "Every switch at its cheapest" is not true of the all-favourable example (losses
0.6% not 0.2%; restart 14 days not 4).

**Wrong numbers in RESULTS.md** (line numbers at `7a3165d`): 99 "−$8.3" for cells that make almost no ammonia or lactate is −$7.7; 85 "gives back $2.5" is
$2.0; 78 "4% of the draws" is 3%; 374 "saves $1.7" against the table's $2.2; 347 "$14.09" is the resulting cost, the step is
$12.34; 101 "growth factors no longer move" the low-lactate example (the switch still adds $2.5–6.5); 105–106 "~12% and ~75%" is 7–22%; 751–752
"10% to 75%" is 5–49%; 745–747 "six premium products" predates the sixteen; 115 and 696 "about 30 years (16 to 74)" is 28
(15–66); 520 "~6%" is 5.4%; 606 the row labelled 13% was computed at 12.8%; 702–708 "$18 / $14 / $53" and "$5 more" are from an
older ladder, and two items are numbered 7; 42 row 1's "$0.30 reported" (the code calls $0.50 the reported price). METHODS.md
1155, 1172, 1222–1241, 1305 and 1362 carry figures from before the spread of liking. `frontier.py:141` still says parity is "about
$7"; `floor_physics.py:5` says $12.51.

**On the page** (full list in the reviewer's report): "Full credit gives ~13% at equal price, and none gives ~13%"; "it follows
from the model's symmetry" beside a 13 against 77 split; "contamination losses: the model does not count them" (it has a switch);
"23.6% is used for every case on this page" and then 17.3%; growth factors "30% of Humbird's price" costing $0.00; the low-lactate example said to
use recycled oxygen in two places and not in its own list; "perfusion in a stirred tank is the cheapest" at $26.43 beside a
bubble-cage cell at $24.73; time to 90% given as 26, 27 and 28 years; worked examples in the methods that print zeros ("near 0% at
launch, ~0% after 10 years and ~0% after 30"); the A4 derivation that does not reproduce its own value; chart labels that round
0.5% to "1%"; "build 2026-11-19 10:29" in the title.

**One sentence at the top**, then one case selector, three numbers and one chart. No second mode; sections open in place.

- **Numbers.** Median first with "8 in 10 draws between"; the most-likely-values figure as a second line; one statistic for cost,
  shop price and share in every row; rounded to the precision of the range. Drawn as a range bar with a dot at the median and a
  tick at the most-likely-values figure.
- **Links.** `a{color:var(--accent);text-decoration:underline;text-decoration-thickness:1px;text-underline-offset:.18em;
  text-decoration-color:color-mix(in srgb,currentColor 40%,transparent)}` with a full underline on hover, the same colour when
  visited and a visible focus ring.
- **Type and layout.** One font in charts and text, chart labels at 11 px or more, paragraphs capped near 70 characters wide, one
  callout style and one tag style. On a phone the cost bar is clipped and its labels are about 7 px.
- **Cut.** The locked company sliders, the chain of four step boxes (it adds a fourth share figure), the donut, the duplicated
  tank paragraph, the worked examples that print zeros, the build stamp.
