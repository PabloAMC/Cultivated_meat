# Cultivated-meat production bioreactors: what has actually been built and reported, 2023-2026

Research date: 2026-11-14. Benchmark for comparison: the cost model's floor assumes 20 m3 (20,000 L) sterile stirred tanks run continuously.

Evidence labels used throughout: **[PR]** peer-reviewed paper; **[PP]** preprint; **[REG]** regulatory filing; **[WP]** company white paper; **[CO]** company claim via press release or interview; **[PRESS]** trade-press reporting, including unnamed sources; **[NGO]** GFI or other independent tracker. Volumes are as stated by the source; almost no source says whether a figure is working or total volume, so assume nominal/total unless marked.

---

## 1. Largest bioreactor installed and operated, by company

### Takeaway
Only one company (Vow, Sydney) has demonstrably run a food-grade vessel at the 20 m3 class, and it runs batch or draw-and-fill, not continuous perfusion. Every other operating company is at or below roughly 2,000 L, and the two plants designed around 20,000 L-plus vessels with perfusion (Believer) or 100,000-250,000 L vessels (UPSIDE Glenview, GOOD Meat/ABEC) never produced commercially.

### Cited Findings

**Vow (Australia) - quail, "Forged"**
- [NGO] GFI's 2026 State of the Industry report (covering 2025) says Vow "achieved production at 20,000-liter scale" in Sydney, in a facility with 35,000 L total capacity and room for 10 more similarly sized lines; GFI calls it the largest current cultivated meat facility in the world and says at this scale Vow can produce "up to low hundreds of tons" annually. — [GFI 2026 SOTI PDF](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GFI-2026-State-of-the-Industry-report-Cultivated-meat-seafood-and-ingredients.pdf)
- [REG] Vow's FSANZ novel-food dossier (application A1269) describes a suspension process in stirred-tank reactors with two harvest options: "Complete harvest: all cells in the STR are harvested" or "Draw and fill: a portion is harvested and a portion is left as cell inoculum to top up the bioreactor again"; it names "2000L STR, 20,000L STR" as the larger vessels seeded as demand requires. Harvest is by concentration and centrifugation. No perfusion or cell-retention step is described. No densities or run lengths are given in the public version. — [Vow FSANZ A1269 dossier](https://consultations.foodstandards.gov.au/sas/a1269-cultured-quail-as-a-novel-food/user_uploads/application---cultured-quail-as-a-novel-food.docx.pdf)
- [CO/PRESS] The 20,000 L "Andromeda" vessel was built in-house, reportedly in under 14 weeks for under US$1 million by ex-SpaceX engineers; Vow claimed a record 538 kg single harvest (reported spring 2025) and targeted 900 kg per harvest, 10,800 kg per month and 130,000 kg per year by end-2025. David Kaplan (Tufts) called it "a great step" but "a drop in the bucket". — [FoodNavigator, 10 Apr 2025](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2025/04/10/cultivated-meat-and-fat-inches-toward-retail/); [Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/vow-cultured-quail-lab-grown-cultivated-meat-australia-forged/)
- [NGO] Vow stated its 20,000 L bioreactor cost 80% less than a similar off-the-shelf vessel. — [GFI 2026 SOTI PDF](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GFI-2026-State-of-the-Industry-report-Cultivated-meat-seafood-and-ingredients.pdf)
- [PRESS, single low-authority source, mostly unsourced] A 2026 analysis says Vow reached a 1,500 kg single harvest and over 5,000 kg per week by June 2026; that the vessel is described as 22,000 L from 2026 (unclear whether the same vessel); that raw cell slurry was reported at under $50/lb in Dec 2024; that Vow cut about 30% of staff (25 people) in Jan 2025 and made further cuts in May 2026 while shifting to co-manufacturing; and that a new CEO took over in June 2026. — [Lifematetech analysis](https://lifematetech.com/articles/vow-production-scaled-strategy-pivoted)
- [PRESS] On 2 July 2026 Parima (ex-Gourmey) reported tonne-scale cultivated duck production in a single run on Vow's 22,000 L line in Sydney. — [Green Queen, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/parima-gourmey-vital-meat-lab-grown-cultivated-production-vow/); [Vegconomist](https://vegconomist.com/manufacturing-technology/parima-reaches-multi-tonne-scale-cultivated-duck-production-vows-bioreactor-line/)

### Inferences
- Distance from the model's assumption, by axis: (a) vessel size, 20 m3 has been reached once (Vow), in batch or draw-and-fill mode; (b) continuous perfusion with cell bleed, demonstrated only at bench scale (Believer paper, SuperMeat 10 L); (c) both together, never demonstrated. The one plant apparently designed for both (Believer, 20,000 L vessels plus GEA perfusion) failed before validating its process.
- Vow's reported harvests imply modest wet-cell titres: 538 kg from a 20,000 L vessel is about 27 g/L if the vessel was full, and 1,500 kg from 22,000 L is about 68 g/L (the latter figure is poorly sourced). These are well below the 430 g/L (43% w/v) reported at bench scale with perfusion by Pasitka et al.
- Vow's process as filed is a food-grade, lower-sterility-spec stirred tank, which is not obviously the same thing as the "sterile stirred tank" in the cost model; its low capital cost claim (under US$1 million for 20,000 L) depends on that difference.
- The step from the largest proven perfusion run (10 L) to 20,000 L is a factor of 2,000 in volume with no published intermediate.

### Gaps
- No public source gives working versus total volume for Vow, Believer, UPSIDE, CellX or Joes Future Food vessels.
- Believer's 20,000 L vessel size rests on AgFunderNews's unnamed-source "understanding"; receiver filings were not retrieved.
- Vessel sizes for Wildtype, BlueNalu's pilot, Aleph Farms, Meatable, Mission Barns and Avant Meats were not found.
- The FDA scientific memos for UPSIDE, GOOD Meat, Wildtype, Mission Barns and Believer, and Singapore SFA documents, were not retrieved; they may state process details.
- Vow's 2026 output figures (1,500 kg harvest, 5,000 kg per week) come from one low-authority article without stated sources.

## 2. Who claims continuous or semi-continuous harvest, and on what evidence

### Takeaway
Continuous harvest with cell retention is supported by one peer-reviewed bench-scale paper (Believer, about 20 days) and one company white paper (SuperMeat, 10 L, 45-day bleed); at production scale the only documented repeated-harvest mode is Vow's draw-and-fill, stated in a regulatory dossier without run-length data.

### Cited Findings
- [PR] Pasitka et al., Nature Food 5, 693-702 (published 21 Aug 2024): tangential flow filtration perfusion of chicken cells gave up to 130 x 10^6 cells/mL, "corresponding to yields of 43% w/v and multiple harvests for over 20 days", in animal-component-free medium at US$0.63/L. The techno-economic analysis is for "a theoretical production facility of 50,000 l" and gives US$6.2/lb. — [Europe PMC abstract record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=DOI:10.1038/s43016-024-01022-w); [Nature Food](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-024-01022-w)
- [PRESS] Secondary coverage: daily biomass harvests; the modelled facility produces 2.14 million kg per year; the work was done with and partly funded by Believer Meats, founded by senior author Yaakov Nahmias. — [Phys.org, Aug 2024](https://phys.org/news/2024-08-cultivated-meat.html)
- [REG-adjacent, supplementary information] The paper's supplementary cost tables compare cell-retention options for the 50,000 L facility: 50 ATF-10 units (US$17.5 million), 10 TFF units (US$1.75 million) or 2 large-scale units (US$2.42 million). — [Nature Food supplementary information](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-024-01022-w)
- [WP] SuperMeat (Nov 2024, 23-page report, not peer reviewed): 10 L expansion bioreactor with TFF perfusion reached 80 x 10^6 cells/mL in 9 days at about 0.52 population doublings per day; perfusion ramped from 1 to 5 vessel volumes per day (VVD), target average 1.5 VVD; then a 45-day "bleeding" phase at 0.35 VVD (described as a 30% bleed rate) at 0.5 doublings per day. An earlier 2 L TFF run held 40-60 x 10^6 cells/mL over 25 days with daily bleeding. — [SuperMeat report](https://supermeat.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/A-Deep-Dive-Into-Cost-Effective-Cultivated-Meat-Production-V.pdf)
- [WP] SuperMeat output: about 33 kg of biomass from the 9-day growth plus 45-day bleed, using one 10 L expansion and two 10 L differentiation bioreactors and 945 L of medium; 7-8 lots per 45-day run; fat differentiation under 1 day, muscle 4 days; differentiated cells weigh 1.75 g per 10^9 cells versus 1.04 g for stem cells. — [SuperMeat report](https://supermeat.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/A-Deep-Dive-Into-Cost-Effective-Cultivated-Meat-Production-V.pdf)
- [WP] SuperMeat's 25,000 L model: 5,000 L perfusion seed reactor, 25,000 L expansion reactor with TFF, 45-day continuous bleed, 56.3-day cycle, 5.7 cycles per year, 5 lines, 3% rejection rate, 6.7 million lb per year, 92.8 million L of medium per year, medium under US$0.5/L. — [SuperMeat report](https://supermeat.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/A-Deep-Dive-Into-Cost-Effective-Cultivated-Meat-Production-V.pdf)
- [NGO comment] GFI's Elliot Swartz called SuperMeat's cost projections "optimistic", noted similarity to Believer's TFF process, and said the field needs more published data. — [AgFunderNews, Nov 2024](https://agfundernews.com/supermeat-offers-glimpse-into-a-future-where-cultivated-meat-can-be-produced-at-scale-for-11-79-lb)
- [REG] Vow: "draw and fill" is a filed operating option in stirred tanks up to 20,000 L (see section 1). — [Vow FSANZ A1269 dossier](https://consultations.foodstandards.gov.au/sas/a1269-cultured-quail-as-a-novel-food/user_uploads/application---cultured-quail-as-a-novel-food.docx.pdf)
- [PRESS] CellRev (UK, closed Aug 2025) had "evidence of 60 days of continuous culturing" on a continuous adherent platform; no scale given. — [FoodNavigator, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/01/22/cultivated-meat-who-is-ceasing-operations-and-why)
- [NGO survey] GFI's bioprocessing survey (30 companies surveyed early 2023, published May 2024): for proliferation, fed-batch was the most common mode, followed by continuous processing and simple batch; perfusion was "less common"; for differentiation, 7 of 8 respondents used perfusion. Stirred tanks were the most common reactor; some used airlift, rocking-bed, fixed-bed or hollow-fibre. — [GFI survey PDF](https://gfi.org/cm-bioprocessing-survey-pdf)

### Inferences
- SuperMeat's 33 kg from 10 L of expansion volume over 54 days is about 60 g of wet (differentiated) biomass per litre of expansion volume per day, or about 20 g/L/day counting all three 10 L vessels. Medium use is about 29 L per kg. These are self-reported and unreplicated.
- Neither continuous-harvest data set says anything about the things that fail at 20 m3: sterility over 45 days with large TFF skids, filter fouling at scale, oxygen transfer and shear at 80-130 x 10^6 cells/mL, and genetic drift over the extra doublings. Believer's collapse with "cell growth at scale not reliable" (unnamed sources) is the only large-scale datapoint, and it is negative.
- The claim in the brief that the Pasitka run was at 5 L could not be confirmed from accessible sources; the abstract and press coverage give no vessel volume. Treat the scale as "bench, single-digit litres" with about 80% confidence.

### Gaps
- Pasitka et al. full text was paywalled; vessel working volume, harvest fraction per day and perfusion rate were not verified.
- No company has published a perfusion or continuous run at 100 L or above for cultivated meat. Whether Believer ever ran its 2,000 L or 20,000 L vessels in perfusion is unknown.
- UPSIDE's and GOOD Meat's operating modes at 2,000-6,000 L (likely fed-batch) were not confirmed from primary filings.

## 3. Peer-reviewed or preprint work at pilot scale (100 L and above), 2023-2026

### Takeaway
I found no peer-reviewed or preprint paper from 2023-2026 reporting cultivated-meat cell culture at 100 L or more with densities, doubling times and run lengths; the public literature tops out at about 3-10 L, and all larger-scale information is company claims.

### Cited Findings
- [PR] Pasitka et al. 2024 (Nature Food): bench-scale TFF perfusion, 130 x 10^6 cells/mL, over 20 days (see section 2). — [Nature Food](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-024-01022-w)
- [PR] Scientific Reports 2025: bovine satellite cells on microcarriers scaled only to a 3 L benchtop bioreactor; doubling time about 50 h; 50,000-60,000 cells/cm2. — [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97813-7)
- [PP] IntegriCulture, bioRxiv 8 Jan 2026: packed-bed bioreactor for adherent duck liver cells; seven trials over seven months in 2025; cell doubling time 36.8 h in flasks; in the packed bed, growth plateaued at 2.88-fold, only 21.1% of the theoretical 13.65-fold; batch size 3.6 x 10^9 cells. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.08.698307v1.full.pdf)
- [NGO] GFI/BioPharm Designs technical report (2025) on cell growth modelling says scaling "will require very large bioreactors", that these will have heterogeneous environments and rising viscosity at high cell concentration, and recommends that researchers report specific growth rates under bioreactor conditions; its empirical anchors for continuous culture are Pasitka et al. 2024 and the SuperMeat report. — [GFI technical report, Oct 2025](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Technical-report-Optimizing-CM-Technoeconomics-2025.pdf)
- [NGO survey] GFI survey (data from early 2023): larger producers "aim for" harvest densities often above 50 x 10^6 cells/mL and occasionally up to 400 x 10^6 cells/mL; these are targets, not reported results. Bioreactors of a few hundred to about 1,000 L cost several hundred thousand to about a million dollars. — [GFI survey PDF](https://gfi.org/cm-bioprocessing-survey-pdf)
- [NGO] GFI 2026: at least 13 original research papers (including preprints) in 2025 came from teams with industry affiliations, up from a previous high of nine in 2023. — [GFI 2026 SOTI PDF](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GFI-2026-State-of-the-Industry-report-Cultivated-meat-seafood-and-ingredients.pdf)
- [Consultancy] Arthur D. Little (2025 viewpoint): cultivated meat production is "largely unproven above 10 cubic meter scale reactors"; in one pilot process design, six bioreactors of about 3 m3 working volume delivered about 90% of the scale effect. Quoted from a search summary; the PDF itself could not be downloaded. — [Arthur D. Little](https://www.adlittle.com/en/insights/viewpoints/cultivated-meat-sustainable-profitable-protein-revolution)
- [CO] Airlift: Ark Biotech's 1,250 L run (late 2023) is unpublished; no density or duration reported. — [AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/cultivated-meat-in-the-spotlight-at-tufts-cellular-ag-day-focusing-on-science-experiments-that-cannot-scale-is-misleading-people)
- [CO] Hollow-fibre: Cellular Agriculture Ltd reports runs but publishes no data; an Innovate UK project with Campden BRI (announced Feb 2024) aims to validate scalability. — [Food and Drink Technology](https://www.foodanddrinktechnology.com/news/51223/innovate-uk-backs-campden-bri-and-cellular-agriculture-with-funding-for-cultivated-meat-project/)

### Inferences
- The model's 20 m3 continuous floor is extrapolated from data three to four orders of magnitude smaller. Probability that a peer-reviewed cultivated-meat run at 1,000 L or above with density and duration data exists and was missed by this search: about 15%.
- Reported bench doubling times cluster at roughly 33-50 h (SuperMeat 0.5 doublings per day is 48 h during bleed; 0.67 per day is 36 h in propagation; IntegriCulture 36.8 h; bovine satellite cells 50 h). Models that assume about 24 h are more optimistic than anything published for food-grade lines in bioreactors.

### Gaps
- Searches for Biotechnology and Bioengineering, npj Science of Food and Trends in Biotechnology papers on pilot-scale airlift, hollow-fibre or aggregate culture returned nothing at 100 L or above; a direct PubMed or Scholar query was not run.
- Believer's "hands-on experience at 300 L" is asserted in the Nature Food paper per AgFunderNews, with no data.

## 4. What has gone wrong: cancellations, bankruptcies, contamination, scale-up failures

### Takeaway
Every attempt to build above about 20,000 L per vessel has been cancelled or has failed before commercial production, and the proximate cause is usually financing, with unnamed-source reports at Believer and UPSIDE pointing to unreliable cell growth and contamination underneath.

### Cited Findings
- **Believer Meats.** Gray Construction says it completed the Wilson plant in Aug 2025; FDA "no questions" letter July 2025; forbearance agreement Oct 2025 requiring US$22 million by 5 Dec; USDA grant of inspection 31 Oct 2025; mass layoffs 1 Dec 2025; payment missed 5 Dec; closure announced about 10 Dec 2025. Gray sued for about US$34 million (US$36.4 million in other reports); Ameris Bank held a US$25 million secured loan. — [Protein Production Technology](https://proteinproductiontechnology.com/post/believer-meats-collapses-despite-major-regulatory-wins-and-us-390-million-in-funding); [Green Queen, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/believer-meats-cultivated-lab-grown-meat-shuts-down-lawsuit/); [AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/exclusive-believer-meats-creditors-mull-credit-bid-after-upside-foods-exit)
- **Believer, causes** [PRESS, unnamed sources]: "The technology should never have been scaled the way it was, because it wasn't working at small scale"; cell growth at scale was not reliable and contamination was a concern; technical issues delayed process validation; about US$390 million raised created pressure to build big before the process was set. — [AgFunderNews, 17 Dec 2025](https://agfundernews.com/what-went-wrong-at-believer-meats-sources-point-to-risk-scale-and-timing)
- **Believer, aftermath.** US subsidiary placed in receivership; UPSIDE Foods submitted a US$50 million stalking-horse bid for the plant (reported mid-2026), then terminated it (reported by 31 Aug 2026) while "remaining interested"; creditors weighed a credit bid; the plant was unsold at last report. — [AgFunderNews bid](https://agfundernews.com/exclusive-upside-foods-submits-50m-stalking-horse-bid-for-believer-meats-us-cultivated-meat-facility); [AgFunderNews withdrawal](https://agfundernews.com/breaking-upside-foods-drops-bid-for-believer-meats-us-cultivated-meat-facility); [Biofuels Digest](https://biofuelsdigest.com/?p=1006584)
- **UPSIDE Foods.** Glenview plant (up to 100,000 L cultivators) paused Feb 2024; layoffs Feb 2024, July 2024 and a third round within a year. Wired (Sept 2023) reported roller-bottle production and frequent contamination losses in custom bioreactors. — [Food Processing](https://www.foodprocessing.com/ingredients/alternative-protein/news/33037213/upside-foods-halts-plan-for-new-illinois-cultivated-meat-plant); [Vegconomist](https://vegconomist.com/company-news/upside-foods-restructures-operations-amid-ongoing-workforce-reductions/); [TABLE Debates](https://tabledebates.org/research-library/insiders-reveal-major-problems-lab-grown-meat-startup-upside-foods)

- **Meatable** (Netherlands, pork): dissolution announced 19-22 Dec 2025 after failing to raise capital; a planned Singapore pilot with TruMeat was never funded. — [Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/meatable-lab-grown-meat-cultivated-pork-startup-funding-shuts-down/); [NL Times, 22 Dec 2025](https://nltimes.nl/2025/12/22/cultivated-meat-startup-meatable-shuts-failing-secure-new-funding)
- **Other closures**: SCiFi Foods (mid-2024; cell lines acquired by GFI/Tufts); CellRev (Aug 2025); Upstream Foods (mid-2025); Cell4Food (Portugal, late 2025); Cultimate Foods (Germany, bankruptcy then closure, reported 2026). — [FoodNavigator, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/01/22/cultivated-meat-who-is-ceasing-operations-and-why); [Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/cultimate-foods-lab-grown-cultivated-meat-fat-startup-bankruptcy-closure/)
- **Vow**: about 30% of staff cut in Jan 2025; further cuts reported May 2026. — [Green Queen via Lifematetech](https://lifematetech.com/articles/vow-production-scaled-strategy-pivoted)
- **Consolidation**: Umami Bioworks merged with Shiok Meats (2024); Gourmey merged with Vital Meat to form PARIMA (2025); Fork & Good acquired Orbillion; Nexture Bio acquired Matrix F.T. — [GFI 2026 SOTI PDF](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GFI-2026-State-of-the-Industry-report-Cultivated-meat-seafood-and-ingredients.pdf)
- **Sector funding**: US$73.9 million raised in 2025 (US$3.4 billion cumulative since 2016); company count 142 in 2025, down from 155 in 2024. — [GFI 2026 SOTI PDF](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GFI-2026-State-of-the-Industry-report-Cultivated-meat-seafood-and-ingredients.pdf)

### Inferences
- The failure pattern is "capital committed to large steel before the bioprocess was validated at intermediate scale" (Believer, GOOD Meat, UPSIDE Glenview). Vow is the counter-example: cheap in-house steel, premium low-volume product, batch or draw-and-fill.
- No public regulatory finding of contamination at a cultivated-meat plant was found; contamination evidence is anecdotal (former staff, unnamed sources).

### Gaps
- No published batch-failure or contamination rates for any company at any scale.
- The exact dates of UPSIDE's bid and withdrawal, and the final outcome of the Believer receivership sale, were not confirmed from primary court documents.

## 5. Independent trackers: installed capacity, largest vessels, scale-up status, recent TEAs

### Takeaway
GFI does not publish a total installed bioreactor capacity for the sector; its 2026 report says most companies operate below 1,000 L, only a handful have commissioned larger facilities, the largest operating facility is Vow's 35,000 L, and no new peer-reviewed techno-economic model appeared in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- [NGO] "Currently, most companies operate bioreactors with volumes below 1,000 liters, with only a handful commissioning facilities capable of larger processes." — [GFI 2026 SOTI PDF](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GFI-2026-State-of-the-Industry-report-Cultivated-meat-seafood-and-ingredients.pdf)
- [NGO] GFI 2026 says "bioreactor capacity in operation" expanded globally in 2025, citing Vow at 20,000 L and others expanding pilot-scale campaigns, but gives no aggregate figure. — [GFI 2026 SOTI PDF](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GFI-2026-State-of-the-Industry-report-Cultivated-meat-seafood-and-ingredients.pdf)
- [NGO] On cost evidence: TEAs commissioned by Gourmey (PARIMA) and Aleph Farms both "optimistically" assess cost at 5,000 L scale, but "several sensitive details were omitted, making them difficult to independently verify"; "With no new peer-reviewed techno-economic models published in 2025, the true implementation cost of cultivated meat remains difficult to pinpoint"; there is "a lack of reliable data points for the cost of equipment, facilities, and other infrastructure". — [GFI 2026 SOTI PDF](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GFI-2026-State-of-the-Industry-report-Cultivated-meat-seafood-and-ingredients.pdf)
- [NGO] Media: several companies announced media costs at or below US$0.20/L in 2025; a GFI/MG Consulting amino-acid supply-chain analysis found food-grade amino acid costs support media below US$0.20/L. — [GFI 2026 SOTI PDF](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/GFI-2026-State-of-the-Industry-report-Cultivated-meat-seafood-and-ingredients.pdf)
- [NGO] GFI's earlier report (covering 2023) noted a 20,000 L bioreactor "coming online soon", the largest production scale reported to date. Taken from a search snippet; the report was not opened. — [GFI 2024 SOTI summary](https://gfi.org/2024-cultivated-state-of-the-industry-summary-pdf)
- [NGO survey] GFI bioprocessing survey (May 2024; data early 2023): production "currently relies on bioreactors in the low-thousand-liter range"; of 25 respondents, 12 planned to scale up, 3 to scale out, the rest both; respondents expected cost-competitiveness at capacities of thousands of tonnes and proliferation vessels in the tens of thousands of litres; 7 of 10 companies planned stirred tanks for future facilities. — [GFI survey PDF](https://gfi.org/cm-bioprocessing-survey-pdf); [Vegconomist summary](https://vegconomist.com/market-and-trends/new-gfi-report-reveals-current-trends-in-cultivated-meat-production-scaling)
- [NGO] GFI/BioPharm Designs 2025 growth-modelling report: a framework and research-priority document, not a new TEA with company cost data. — [GFI technical report](https://gfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Technical-report-Optimizing-CM-Technoeconomics-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- A rough bottom-up tally of the largest publicly reported operating vessels as of late 2026 (Vow 35,000 L total; UPSIDE, CellX, Joes Future Food at 2,000 L class; Mosa/Cultivate at Scale and Simple Planet at 1,000 L class; idle 20,000 L-class vessels at the Believer plant) suggests operating capacity dedicated to cultivated meat is of the order of 50,000-100,000 L worldwide, with Vow about a third to a half of it. This is my estimate, not a tracker figure; confidence is low (70% that the true figure is between 40,000 and 200,000 L).

### Gaps
- No tracker (GFI or otherwise) was found that publishes total installed litres. GFI's 2025 edition (covering 2024) was not opened.
- The Lever VC cost-progress overview cited by GFI was not retrieved.

- The one figure anchored to a real 20 m3-class run (PARIMA, low EUR 40s/kg finished product, batch mode, partly plant-based) is roughly 3-6 times the modelled 5,000-50,000 L claims. That gap is a reasonable prior for the distance between current practice and the modelled floor.
- Cost per kg of pure wet cell mass is not recoverable from the Gourmey, Aleph or PARIMA figures because inclusion rates and scaffold or plant-protein costs are not disclosed.

### Gaps
- No company has published audited cost of goods. Vow's current per-kg cost is not public.
- The ADL 2025 viewpoint and the Green Queen 2 July 2026 article were not read in full; figures for PARIMA's run come through secondary summaries.
- Believer's internal cost at the Wilson plant is unknown.
