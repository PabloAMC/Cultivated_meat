# Continuous and semi-continuous animal-cell culture in biopharma: state of the art (2026) and relevance to 20-50 m3 vessels

Researcher notes, compiled 2026-11-14. Evidence labels used throughout: **[DEMONSTRATED]** = reported practice; **[VENDOR]** = supplier claim, not independently verified; **[MODEL/PROPOSAL]** = design study, patent or techno-economic assumption. Search coverage was uneven: several primary sources (PMC full texts, Thieme, Europe PMC) could not be opened, and one search on veterinary-vaccine BHK processes was blocked by the search tool. Items I could not source in this session are listed under Gaps, including several well-known figures I recall but could not verify.

## 1. Largest vessel volumes actually run with animal cells, by mode

### Takeaway
Batch/fed-batch suspension culture is demonstrated to 20-25 m3; perfusion with cell retention is demonstrated commercially only to about 2-4 m3 (4,000 L perfusion bioreactors at Genzyme Geel, c. 2005; an 8,000 L build announced 2011 whose mode I could not confirm), and vendor ratings stop at 5,000 L. I found no documented animal-cell perfusion, chemostat or fill-and-draw production run above 10 m3, and no evidence that a retention-free chemostat has ever been a commercial animal-cell process.

### Cited Findings

Fed-batch / batch (the reference ceiling)
- [DEMONSTRATED] "The largest bioreactors are 25,000 L for suspension cell culture, and around 5,000 L for microcarrier cell culture" (2012 retrospective). — [BioPharm International, 25-year retrospective](https://biopharminternational.com/view/25-year-retrospective-mammalian-cell-culture)
- [DEMONSTRATED] Vacaville site listed with 12,000 L and 25,000 L bioreactors; Fujifilm Holly Springs opened with eight 20,000 L stainless-steel mammalian bioreactors and plans eight more. The article contrasts "a 5,000-L disposable reactor" with "a 20,000-L or 25,000-L stainless-steel vessel". — [Pharma's Almanac, "Is the bioreactor arms race over"](https://www.pharmasalmanac.com/nice-insight/articles/is-the-bioreactor-arms-race-over)
- [DEMONSTRATED] Humbird: therapeutic proteins are made in "stainless-steel bioreactors up to ~20 m3". — [Humbird 2021, Biotechnol Bioeng 118:3239](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8362201)
- [DEMONSTRATED] A 25,000 L fed-batch CHO process (BMS) was the subject of a scale-down study; differences at 25,000 L were traced mainly to lower viability, with CO2 stripping and base consumption probed as causes. — [AIChE 2013 abstract 660e](https://proceedings.aiche.org/conferences/aiche-annual-meeting/2013/proceeding/paper/660e-systematic-development-scale-down-model-25000-l-fed-batch-cho-culture-process-1)
- [DEMONSTRATED] A 1999 review of large-scale suspension culture noted that no specific design guidelines existed for scales of 10,000 L and above, so designs borrowed from microbial fermentation (search-snippet level; full text not opened). — [PMC3463394](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3463394)
- [DEMONSTRATED] Single-use ceiling: WuXi Biologics operates 4,000 L single-use bioreactors (twelve at its Irish site), has installed 5,000 L single-use units at Hangzhou and plans 6,000 L units; batches "from 4,000 L to 16,000 L" are scale-out of several SUBs and are described as fed-batch, not perfusion. — [European Pharmaceutical Review](https://www.europeanpharmaceuticalreview.com/news/229968/wuxi-facility-boosts-bioprocessing-capacity-with-new-bioreactors/); [GEN](https://genengnews.com/topics/bioprocessing/wuxi-biologics-boosts-manufacturing-capability-with-installation-of-three-5000l-single-use-bioreactors)

Perfusion with cell retention
- [DEMONSTRATED] Genzyme Geel (Belgium): 2003-2005 expansion added "two new 4,000 l perfusion bioreactors" for lysosomal-storage-disorder enzymes (Myozyme/Lumizyme); another 4,000 L bioreactor added from 2009; a EUR 250m facility started January 2011 was to be "equipped with 8,000 L bioreactors" (mode not stated on the page). The page does not name the retention device. — [Pharmaceutical Technology, Genzyme Geel project page](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/projects/genzyme/)
- [DEMONSTRATED] Genzyme Allston (Cerezyme, Fabrazyme; CHO): six bioreactors; a 2009 virus contamination in one of the six halted the plant; Myozyme production was at "a 4,000 liter facility". — [GEN, Genzyme halts production](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/bioprocessing/genzyme-temporarily-halts-production-at-plant-in-massachusetts/); [news-medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20091117/Genzyme-receives-FDA-complete-response-letter-for-its-Lumizyme-marketing-application.aspx)
- [DEMONSTRATED] Bayer Kogenate (rFVIII, BHK suspension, Berkeley): continuous perfusion used "for over two decades"; a fermentation campaign "can be months in duration". — [Bayer, "A regulatory perspective on continuous perfusion production of rFVIII", ECI](https://dc.engconfintl.org/biomanufact_ii/31). In 2002 the FDA approved six 200 L fermenters at Berkeley, "twice the volume" of those then in use (i.e. roughly 100 L previously). — [Manufacturing Chemist](https://manufacturingchemist.com/fda-approves-bayer-manufacturing-processes-32618). Market requirement was put at about 150 g rFVIII per year, which is why such small vessels sufficed. — [US patent application 20140099711](https://uspto.report/patent/app/20140099711)
- [DEMONSTRATED] Janssen Remicade (infliximab): label states the product is "produced by a recombinant cell line cultured by continuous perfusion". Volumes not given. — [Drugs.com Remicade prescribing information](https://www.Drugs.Com/pro/remicade.html)
- [DEMONSTRATED] A 3,000 L N-1 perfusion bioreactor with an inclined settler reached up to 15.8 x 10^6 viable cells/mL (Pohlscheidt; seed stage, not production); the same source notes inclined settlers struggle at higher densities because of perfusion-rate limits and long cell residence times. — [BioProcess International, seed-train article](https://bioprocessintl.com/single-use/a-novel-seed-train-process-using-high-density-cell-banking-a-disposable-bioreactor-and-perfusion-technologies)
- [DEMONSTRATED] Trade summary (2016): perfusion typically uses ~2,000 L bioreactors "even for commercial production", versus ~20,000 L for fed-batch. — [BioPharm International, "Perfusion in the 21st Century"](https://www.biopharminternational.com/view/perfusion-21st-century)
- [DEMONSTRATED] 2023/2024 peer-reviewed statement: "multiple parallel ATF systems were required to operate 2000 L perfusion bioreactors"; 2,000 L is treated as manufacturing scale. — [Romann et al. 2024, Biotechnol Bioeng 121:640](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.28589)
- [VENDOR] WuXiUP continuous process: GMP at 200 L (2018), pilot-scale automated continuous drug substance in 2025; WuXi claims 1,000-2,000 L single-use perfusion is "comparable to traditional 10,000-20,000 L stainless steel bioreactors". No WuXiUP run at 4,000-5,000 L found. — [Pharma Manufacturing](https://www.pharmamanufacturing.com/sector/contract-manufacturing/news/55309827/wuxi-biologics-achieves-pilot-scale-automated-continuous-drug-substance-production); [Contract Pharma 2018](https://www.contractpharma.com/contents/view_breaking-news/2018-12-12/wuxi-biologics-advances-cell-culture-productivity/53327)
- [VENDOR] Repligen says XCell ATF "has been scaled up to 5000 L production bioreactors"; ATF 10 rated for 200-5,000 L working volume. — [Repligen white paper 2022](https://www.repligen.com/_default_upload_bucket/Animal_Vaccine_Mnfg_Whitepaper_20JUL2022.pdf); [Repligen size guide](https://www.repligen.com/technologies/xcell-atf/XCell-ATF-Devices-and-Controllers)
- [MODEL/PROPOSAL] A patent disclosure states perfusion "can now be scaled up to bioreactors having a volume up to 1000 liters". — [US 9,745,548, acoustic perfusion devices](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9745548)

Chemostat (no retention) and fill-and-draw
- [DEMONSTRATED, lab scale only] Chemostat culture of mammalian cells dates to the early 1960s (Pirt and Callow 1964; 1961 J Exp Med chemostat). A 1973 study ran steady-state chemostats of mouse LS cells at dilution rates of 0.25-0.35 per day. — [J Gen Virol 20:29](https://www.microbiologyresearch.org/content/journal/jgv/10.1099/0022-1317-20-1-29)
- [DEMONSTRATED, lab scale] Concentrated HeLa/KB/HEp-2 chemostats in a dialysis fermentor ran up to 40 days at ~1.2 x 10^6 cells/mL (1965). — [PMC1058198](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1058198)
- [DEMONSTRATED, scale unstated] Wellcome's Namalwa lymphoblastoid interferon ("Wellferon") process, devised at Beckenham in 1975, grew cells "in large stainless steel tanks" for commercial production; licensed for trials 1980-81. Mode and volume not given in the retrieved source. — [Finter et al. review, PMC7100942 copy](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/d83b3881c9bf6c6187a855f0fd208fb5/PMC7100942.pdf)
- [MODEL/PROPOSAL] Draw-fill is described in a patent as closely resembling repeated batch, with typically 80% of tank volume removed per cycle; CHO, BHK, HEK293 named as suitable. Patent text, not evidence of commercial practice. — [US 9,212,214](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9212214)

### Inferences
- The demonstrated ladder is roughly: fed-batch 25 m3 >> perfusion production 4 m3 (possibly 8 m3 planned) > vendor-rated retention hardware 5 m3 > routinely cited perfusion scale 1-2 m3. The cost model's 20 m3 continuous vessel sits 5-10x above any documented continuous animal-cell production vessel.
- The large historic perfusion processes (Kogenate at 100-200 L; enzymes at 4,000 L) existed because the product was labile or needed in gram-to-kilogram quantities. They were not optimised for biomass output, so they are weak precedents for a biomass process.
- A retention-free chemostat does not face the retention-device ceiling, so in vessel-engineering terms it scales like a fed-batch tank (20-25 m3 is proven hardware). What is undemonstrated is the operating mode (weeks of continuous sterile feed and bleed), not the vessel.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the retention device on the Genzyme 4,000 L perfusion reactors (I recall gravity settlers/microcarrier retention but did not source it), nor whether the 8,000 L Geel bioreactors run in perfusion.
- Could not source: Janssen/Centocor perfusion volumes (I recall 500-1,000 L with spin filters), Kogenate cell densities and exact run length (I recall runs of up to ~6 months with settlers), Wellcome's 8,000 L Namalwa tanks, Celltech's 10,000 L airlift hybridoma reactors, Samsung 15,000 L and Lonza 20,000 L vessel sizes. All are recalled from training knowledge and unverified here; treat as leads, not facts.
- No retrievable source on veterinary-vaccine BHK-21 suspension processes run as repeated batch in multi-m3 tanks (search blocked). This is probably the best historical precedent for large fill-and-draw animal-cell culture and should be checked by hand (e.g. Radlett et al. 1985; Capstick-era literature).
- No data found for insect-cell or Vero continuous culture above pilot scale.

## 2. Run lengths in practice, what ends runs, and failure/contamination rates

### Takeaway
Commercial perfusion runs last 30-100+ days (historically 2-3 months; rFVIII campaigns "months"), and are ended mainly by filter fouling/device failure, planned campaign limits tied to validated cell age, and contamination. Survey data put facility-level batch failure at roughly once every 9-15 months, with contamination the leading cause at >=1,000 L, but no public dataset breaks failure rate out by run length or by perfusion versus fed-batch.

### Cited Findings
- Perfusion runs "often run 30-100 days" against 10-14 days for fed-batch (2016). — [BioPharm International](https://www.biopharminternational.com/view/perfusion-21st-century)
- Historically perfusion was "a two- or three-month process"; with spin filters, "once its screen fouls, the run is terminated"; gravity settlers lose cells at high recirculation and expose cells to a suboptimal external environment at larger sizes; an ATF failure need not end the run because the device can be swapped sterilely. — [BioProcess International, "A Brief History of Perfusion Biomanufacturing"](https://bioprocessintl.com/bioreactors/a-brief-history-of-perfusion-biomanufacturing)
- TFF retention devices "must be operational for as much as several months (without cleaning)" at low fluxes of about 2 L/m2/h; "filter clogging and product retention remain major challenges"; diaphragm-pump ATF was "associated with operational instability at the manufacturing scale". — [Romann et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.28589)
- rFVIII campaign duration "can be months". — [Bayer/ECI](https://dc.engconfintl.org/biomanufact_ii/31)
- BioPlan 5th annual survey (2008): one batch failure per facility every 40.6 weeks (145 organisations). Total failure rate 7.6% for >1,000 L versus 6.6% for <1,000 L; contamination was the top cause at >=1,000 L (2.3%); larger facilities had about 3x the contamination rate of smaller ones. The article also states scale did not differentiate overall failure rates much. — [BioProcess International](https://bioprocessintl.com/bioanalytical-methods/biotech-facilities-average-a-batch-failure-every-40-6-weeks)
- Later BioPlan figures quoted second-hand: about 58 weeks between failures (2022), and a 64-week figure of unclear edition. — [Cytiva, reduce batch loss](https://www.cytivalifesciences.com/en/us/insights/reduce-batch-loss)
- BioPlan 22nd annual report (2025): commercial-scale facilities report contamination (e.g. bacterial) among top causes; clinical-scale report more equipment failure; some sites have nearly 4x the failures of others. — [BioPharm International, Dec 2025](https://www.biopharminternational.com/view/biopharma-by-the-numbers-batch-failures-in-biopharma-manufacturing)
- Perception data: in a 2011 BioPlan survey 63.9% had contamination-risk concerns about perfusion versus 3.8% for fed-batch; only about 10% of facilities use perfusion upstream. — [BioProcess International, continuous biomanufacturing](https://www.bioprocessintl.com/perfusion-cell-culture/continuous-biomanufacturing-and-perfusion-technologies-are-finally-on-the-cusp-of-broad-adoption)
- [MODEL] Lim et al. 2006 (UCL) assumed 10% probabilities of contamination and of fouling for spin-filter perfusion and found they had to fall to 3% for perfusion to win on reward/risk. — [Lim et al. 2006, Biotechnol Bioeng](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.20757)
- [MODEL] Pollock, Ho and Farid 2013: fed-batch was more robust under failure-rate uncertainty; competitiveness of perfusion depends on equipment failure rates. — [Pollock et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.24608); [Pollock et al. 2017](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1556886/1/Farid_Pollock_et_al-2017-Biotechnology_Progress.pdf)
- Viral contamination: the CAACB industry study (20 manufacturers) reported 18 virus contamination events in mammalian cell culture since 1985; a later MIT CBI paper cites 26 events for 1985-2020 "relative to thousands of batches per year". Counts conflict between sources. — [MIT News on Barone et al. 2020](https://news.mit.edu/2020/contamination-drug-manufacturing-plants-0427); [MIT CBI 2023](https://cbi.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Historical-Evaluation-of-in-vivo.pdf)
- A single virus event can idle a plant: Genzyme Allston 2009, one of six bioreactors contaminated, whole plant shut for sanitisation. — [GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/bioprocessing/genzyme-temporarily-halts-production-at-plant-in-massachusetts/)
- Genetic drift: CHO cells under continuous sub-cultivation up to 220 generations showed accelerated genetic drift/directed evolution (PhD thesis). — [Fernandez Martell thesis, White Rose](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/9152/7/PhD%20thesis%20Alejandro%20Fernandez%20Martell%20%28final%20version%29.pdf)
- Humbird assumes perfusion uptime of about 91% with ten 72-hour turnarounds per year (i.e. runs of roughly 33 days). — [Humbird 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8362201)

### Inferences
- If per-batch failure is of order 5-8% for 10-14 day fed-batch at >=1,000 L and contamination hazard is roughly proportional to time and to the number of aseptic interventions, a 30-60 day continuous run would carry a materially higher per-run loss probability (order 10-30%) unless hazard per day is lower in closed continuous operation. This is my extrapolation; no survey reports it directly. Confidence: low-to-moderate.
- A retention-free chemostat removes the fouling failure mode entirely, leaving contamination, equipment failure and population drift. At doubling times near 24 h, a 30-day run adds about 30 generations on top of the seed train, well within the 220-generation range where drift is documented, but selection in a chemostat favours fast growers, which is benign or helpful for a biomass product (unlike a protein product where non-producers take over).
- Pharma-grade failure rates are achieved with pharma-grade aseptic design and cleanrooms. A food-grade plant relaxing these would likely do worse, and Humbird's capital costs already assume ASME BPE construction.

### Gaps
- No public breakdown of failure or contamination rate by run length, or perfusion versus fed-batch, at the same scale. BioPlan primary reports are paywalled; all figures here are second-hand.
- No quantified contamination rate per reactor-day for continuous animal-cell culture at >1 m3.
- 18-versus-26 discrepancy in viral event counts not resolved against the Barone et al. 2020 full text.

## 3. Chemostat and fill-and-draw at manufacturing scale: history, densities, productivities

### Takeaway
I found no documented commercial animal-cell process run as a true retention-free chemostat; chemostats appear only at lab scale, at around 10^6 cells/mL and dilution rates of 0.25-0.35 per day. Fill-and-draw (repeated batch) is described as standard practice in patents and seed trains, but I could not retrieve a sourced production-scale example with volumes and densities.

### Cited Findings
- Lab chemostats of mammalian cells: dilution rates 0.25-0.35 per day (mouse LS cells, 1973); ~1.2 x 10^6 cells/mL for up to 40 days (HeLa, 1965, dialysis-assisted). — [J Gen Virol 1973](https://www.microbiologyresearch.org/content/journal/jgv/10.1099/0022-1317-20-1-29); [PMC1058198](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1058198)
- A CHO continuous-culture study reported lysed cells accumulating to 8 x 10^5 cells/mL after 200 h, attributed to agitation. — [BMC Proceedings 5(S8):P101](https://bmcproc.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/1753-6561-5-S8-P101.pdf)
- [PROPOSAL] Patent family on "continuous cell culture" for polypeptide/virus production describes CHO cultures at low dilution rates with mean specific growth rate about 0.22 per day. — [US 9,650,612](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9650612)
- Draw-fill defined as removing typically 80% of the tank and refilling. — [US 9,212,214](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9212214)
- The commercial "continuous" animal-cell processes that are documented all use retention: Kogenate (BHK, perfusion), Remicade (perfusion), Genzyme enzymes (4,000 L perfusion). — [Bayer/ECI](https://dc.engconfintl.org/biomanufact_ii/31); [Remicade PI](https://www.Drugs.Com/pro/remicade.html); [Pharmaceutical Technology](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/projects/genzyme/)
- Why continuous lost to fed-batch in pharma: fed-batch titres rose (to 4.7 g/L by the 2010s), removing the productivity argument; perfusion persisted for unstable or cytotoxic products. — [BioPharm International 2016](https://www.biopharminternational.com/view/perfusion-21st-century)
- Cultivated-meat precedent closest to continuous harvest: Pasitka et al. 2024 (Nature Food) used TFF perfusion with multiple harvests over more than 20 days, then modelled a 50,000 L facility; this uses retention and the 50,000 L figure is a model, not a run. — [Nahmias lab summary](https://yaakovnahmias.huji.ac.il/node/3188801)
- [VENDOR] One cultivated-meat equipment page advertises a chemostat-style gravity-settling harvest for 1-50,000 L with no performance data. — [Lab-Grown Technologies](https://lab-grown-technologies.super.site/continuous-seed-train-and-light-harvest-system-for-cultured-meat-production)

### Inferences
- In a retention-free chemostat the dilution rate equals the specific growth rate, so with mu_max ~0.029/h (0.7/d) the practical dilution rate is about 0.3-0.6 per day. Steady-state density is then capped by nutrient supply and by-product inhibition at batch-like levels (historically 1-5 x 10^6 cells/mL; plausibly 10-30 x 10^6 with modern concentrated media and low-waste lines). Volumetric biomass productivity is D x X, so at D = 0.5/d each 10 g/L of steady-state wet cell mass yields 5 g/L/day. The model should state explicitly which density it assumes, because pharma offers no demonstrated chemostat density above about 10^6-10^7 cells/mL. Confidence that no commercial retention-free animal-cell chemostat exists in pharma: ~80% (absence of evidence across reviews searched; historical vaccine processes not checked).
- Pharma's lack of chemostats reflects product economics (titre, product quality consistency, batch definition), not a proven physical impossibility. For a biomass product the main objections (non-producer takeover, product heterogeneity) largely fall away; washout risk, sterility over long runs and low steady-state density remain.

### Gaps
- No sourced production-scale repeated-batch example with volume, cell density and number of cycles. Candidates recalled but unverified: BHK-21 veterinary vaccine processes in multi-m3 tanks; Wellcome Namalwa interferon at 8,000 L; hybridoma airlift processes. These need manual library retrieval (e.g. Arathoon and Birch 1986 Science; Griffiths 1992; Kretzmer 2002).
- No g cells/L/day figures for any production-scale continuous animal-cell process were found; pharma reports product titre, not biomass productivity.

## 4. Cell densities in large fed-batch versus perfusion, and what limits density at scale

### Takeaway
Large fed-batch typically peaks around 10-30 x 10^6 cells/mL, while perfusion reaches 50-100+ x 10^6 cells/mL at small-to-mid scale; at 20 m3 and above the binding constraints are CO2 stripping and oxygen transfer under shear-limited sparging and agitation, with ammonia/lactate limiting unmodified cells well before that.

### Cited Findings
- Fed-batch densities: about 5 x 10^6 cells/mL in the mid-1990s, more than 15 x 10^6 by c. 2011; concentrated fed-batch and perfusion above 10^8 cells/mL reported. — [BioProcess International](https://bioprocessintl.com/bioreactors/a-brief-history-of-perfusion-biomanufacturing)
- Perfusion: cell concentrations "generally above 100 x 10^6 vc/mL can be reached", maintained 30 to more than 100 days; volumetric protein productivity above 1.5 g/L/day. — [BioPharm International 2016](https://www.biopharminternational.com/view/perfusion-21st-century)
- N-1 perfusion at 3,000 L with inclined settler: 15.8 x 10^6 cells/mL. — [BioProcess International](https://bioprocessintl.com/single-use/a-novel-seed-train-process-using-high-density-cell-banking-a-disposable-bioreactor-and-perfusion-technologies)
- Humbird's limits: viscosity ceiling at cell volume fraction ~0.25, i.e. 86 x 10^6/mL or 258 g/L wet; fed-batch with metabolically enhanced cells ~110 g/L before ammonia inhibition; "Bioreactors >20 m3 have a lower maximum cell density due to CO2 inhibition"; perfusion ~195 g/L (65 x 10^6/mL) at 1 RV/d, limited by O2 transfer; wild-type cells only ~7 g/L in fed-batch and ~20 g/L (6.8 x 10^6/mL) in perfusion at 2 RV/d, limited by ammonia. — [Humbird 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8362201)
- Humbird's engineering constraints: superficial gas velocity capped at 0.006 m/s (about 0.1 vvm in 20 m3); agitation capped by a turbulent eddy scale near 20 um; pCO2 must stay within 40-100 mbar; because RQ is about 1, CO2 stripping cannot be decoupled from O2 supply; inhibitor limits 5 mM ammonia and 50 mM lactate; his sensitivity analysis finds an optimum near 50 m3 because CO2 limits erode density beyond that. — [Humbird 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8362201)
- Measured at 15,000 L: CO2 mass transfer does not scale like O2; a modified strategy raised CO2 transfer by up to 25% at industrial scale. — [Frontiers in Chemical Engineering 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fceng.2024.1470991/full)
- Matching maximum oxygen transfer capacity across scales can push a stirred tank into excessive hydromechanical stress. — [J Biol Eng 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13036-024-00475-8)
- Perfusion at high density raises the question of whether a large vessel can meet oxygen demand at ~10x normal cell concentration. — [BioProcess International](https://bioprocessintl.com/bioreactors/a-brief-history-of-perfusion-biomanufacturing)
- Counter-claim (industry advocacy, unverified): a Lever VC report says cultivated-meat companies have shown 60-90 g/L, above Humbird's fed-batch ceiling. — [Vegconomist](https://vegconomist.com/meat/report-cultivated-meat-proving-doubters-wrong-increasingly-viable/)

### Inferences
- A retention-free chemostat is density-limited like a batch culture in terms of inhibitors, because spent medium leaves only with the cells. At D ~0.5/d the medium exchange is 0.5 RV/d, lower than Humbird's perfusion cases, so ammonia/lactate limits bite harder than in perfusion unless the cell line is metabolically engineered. Assuming perfusion-like densities (50-100 x 10^6/mL) in a retention-free 20 m3 tank is not supported by pharma evidence.
- At 20-50 m3, gas transfer physics is the same for chemostat and fed-batch, so Humbird's CO2/O2 ceilings apply directly to the cost model's vessel.

### Gaps
- No published peak-VCD dataset specifically for 15,000-25,000 L commercial runs was retrieved; the 10-30 x 10^6/mL figure for large fed-batch rests on trade-press generalisations.
- Humbird cites Clincke et al. 2013 for CHO perfusion densities; not opened here.

## 5. Reviews and vendor claims on scaling retention beyond 2 m3

### Takeaway
Recent literature treats 2,000 L as the manufacturing-scale reference for perfusion and already requires several parallel hollow-fibre devices at that size; vendor ratings top out at 5,000 L (Repligen ATF 10) or 2,000 L (TFDF). Acoustic separators are stuck at around 200-1,000 L/day; inclined settlers and centrifuges are the options usually judged scalable, but with density and residence-time penalties.

### Cited Findings
- Hollow-fibre filtration "has emerged as the dominant technology for cell retention in perfusion processes yet significant challenges in alleviating filter fouling remain unsolved"; TFF (levitated centrifugal pumps) is "the preferred choice within the industry at large scale due to smaller facility footprint and higher robustness"; axial pressure drop forces short modules and "multiple filters in parallel"; only slightly more than 50% of membrane area is effectively used because of Starling recirculation. Coauthors include Levitronix and Merck Serono staff. — [Romann et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.28589)
- [VENDOR] Repligen XCell ATF 10: about 11 m2 filter area, ATF flow 30-80 L/min, rated for 200-5,000 L bioreactors; "scaled up to 5000 L production bioreactors". — [Repligen technology sheet](https://cn.repligen.com/_default_upload_bucket/XCell%20ATF%C2%AE%20Technology.pdf); [Repligen white paper 2022](https://www.repligen.com/_default_upload_bucket/Animal_Vaccine_Mnfg_Whitepaper_20JUL2022.pdf)
- Independent sizing: one ATF 10 handles perfusion "up to 1000 L/d" (Pollock et al. 2013, via Humbird); dual ATFs allow 1 m3 at 2 RV/d or 2 m3 at 1 RV/d, hence "perfusion culture volumes are limited by the capacity of the cell retention device" and Humbird's 96 x 2 m3 perfusion plant design at $51/kg. — [Humbird 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8362201)
- [VENDOR] Repligen KrosFlo TFDF (tangential flow depth filtration, 2-5 um tubular filter): 1-2,000 L, 40-tube 6,000 cm2 module recommended for <2,000 L. — [Repligen TFDF data sheet](https://www.repligen.com/technologies/tfdf/tfdf-technology)
- Acoustic separators were tested up to about 200 L/day; larger units are "very difficult due to the significant power input"; a large inclined settler suitable for a 2,000 L process was tested instead (bleed-recycling study). — [FHNW repository, bleed recycling paper](https://irf.fhnw.ch/bitstreams/c89ee89e-90b8-4699-b46e-83b7e9522721/download)
- 2003 review (Voisard et al.): robust retention is "the major technological limitation in the scaling-up" of perfusion; centrifuges and spin filters had high perfusion capacity, acoustic and inclined settlers good scale-up potential. — [Voisard et al. 2003, Biotechnol Bioeng](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12701141)
- Spin filters have been largely phased out because filter area does not scale with volume and fouling ends runs. — [BioProcess International](https://bioprocessintl.com/bioreactors/a-brief-history-of-perfusion-biomanufacturing)
- [PROPOSAL] Compact/conical settler patents claim examples at 1,000 L and assert a 1,000 L high-density perfusion reactor can out-produce a >20,000 L fed-batch annually. — [US 11,185,799](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11185799)
- GFI's cultivated-meat bioprocess survey: 10 of 15 companies use continuous centrifugation for harvest; some consider 200,000 L or larger vessels (intentions, not practice). — [GFI survey](https://gfi.org/cm-bioprocessing-survey-pdf)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic from the cited sizing: a 20 m3 vessel at 1 RV/d would need about 20 ATF-10-class devices (11 m2 each, ~220 m2 total) recirculating roughly 600-1,600 L/min of culture in aggregate; at 50 m3, about 50. Nothing like this has been reported. This supports the cost model's choice to avoid retention filters at 20 m3, but it means the model cannot borrow perfusion densities.
- For a biomass product, disc-stack centrifugation of the bleed (which is the product) is a harvest step, not a retention step, and is proven at very large flow in other industries; the open question is what happens to medium economics when cells and spent medium leave together.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed report of perfusion above 5,000 L, and no independent data confirming Repligen's 5,000 L statement.
- No Levitronix, Sartorius, Cytiva or Pall document claiming retention for >5,000 L was found; absence may reflect search limits rather than absence of claims.
- 2020-2026 review articles (e.g. Chotteau; Matanguihan and Wu 2022; MacDonald et al. 2022; Coffman et al. 2021) were identified via citation in Romann et al. but not opened.

## 6. Microbial continuous processes at very large scale (labelled comparison)

### Takeaway
Microbial/fungal continuous culture is proven at 155 m3 with run lengths around 1,000 h (Quorn), yet even there runs are terminated by evolution of unwanted variants; it transfers poorly to animal cells because of roughly 10x slower growth, shear sensitivity that caps gas transfer, much lower tolerance to contamination and far richer media.

### Cited Findings
- [MICROBIAL] Quorn (Fusarium venenatum): 155 m3 airlift fermenters operated continuously for about 1,000 h, producing about 2 t/h of mycoprotein. — [Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2023.1204307/full)
- [MICROBIAL] Runs last about a month before short, highly branched "colonial variant" mutants degrade product quality and force a restart; this is "one of the primary barriers to continuous production". — [Microbiology Society 2018](https://microbiologysociety.org/resource/mycoprotein-production-and-food-sustainability.html)
- [MICROBIAL] Quorn 1 was 40 m3; Quorn 2 (155 m3, commissioned late 1993, on the old Pruteen fermenter site) and its twin Quorn 3 are described as the world's largest continuous-flow culture systems: 50 m tall, GBP 37.5M each. — [Moore, 21st Century Guidebook to Fungi, ch. 17.18](https://davidmoore.org.uk/21st_Century_Guidebook_to_Fungi_PLATINUM/Ch17_18.htm)
- Why not transferable (Humbird): animal cells "proliferate much more slowly than microbial cells", "lack a rigid cell wall" so sparging and agitation are limited, and show Warburg-type overflow to lactate and ammonia; vessels need full ASME BPE aseptic design, cleanrooms and virus protection. — [Humbird 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8362201)
- Chemostat dilution rates for mammalian cells were 0.25-0.35 per day in classic studies. — [J Gen Virol 1973](https://www.microbiologyresearch.org/content/journal/jgv/10.1099/0022-1317-20-1-29)

### Inferences
- Quorn runs at dilution rates of order 0.2 per hour (my recollection, unsourced here), i.e. roughly 10-20x the dilution rate available to animal cells; with a doubling time of ~24 h a contaminant bacterium doubling every 30-60 min overtakes an animal-cell chemostat within a day, whereas in a fast microbial chemostat at low pH and simple medium the production organism can out-compete or wash out many invaders. So the "1,000 h at 155 m3" precedent demonstrates large continuous sterile engineering, not that animal cells can hold sterility for 1,000 h at 20 m3.
- Even the best microbial precedent ends runs through genetic variants after ~40 days, suggesting 20-40 days is a sensible upper planning bound for an animal-cell chemostat run before evidence says otherwise.

### Gaps
- ICI Pruteen (I recall a 1,500 m3 pressure-cycle airlift, Methylophilus methylotrophus, late 1970s-1980s, runs of ~100 days) was not sourced in this session beyond the Quorn-site reference.
- Quorn dilution rate and contamination frequency not sourced.

## Overall assessment for the cost model (inference, flagged as such)

- **Vessel**: sterile stirred tanks of 20-25 m3 for suspension animal cells are demonstrated practice (fed-batch). 50 m3 is undemonstrated for animal cells; Humbird treats it as a modelled optimum.
- **Mode**: continuous draw-off without retention at 20 m3 has no documented pharma precedent. Rough credences: a 20 m3 retention-free continuous or fill-and-draw animal-cell process is technically operable for runs of 2-4 weeks: ~65%; that it sustains densities above 50 g/L wet in that mode without major metabolic engineering: ~25%; that pharma-style perfusion with retention is practical at 20 m3 with current hardware: ~10%.
- **Most transferable precedent** is fill-and-draw/repeated batch (it is how seed trains already work, and each cycle is a fed-batch-like event), not the chemostat and not perfusion. The key unknowns for the model are steady-state density without retention, contamination hazard per reactor-day in a food-grade plant, and number of cycles before a forced restart.
