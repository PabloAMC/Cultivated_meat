## 1. Prior art, project by project

Status key: OPEN (no relevant work found), PARTLY (pieces exist, the cultivated-meat synthesis does not), DONE (already published; laptop version adds little).

### A. CO2-optimised reactor shape (shallow, wide, tray, thin-layer)
- **Status: PARTLY, and the core is already in your model.** Your density-limit formula (gas velocity / depth) already says depth is the lever; the tornado already shows tank shape moving cost from $9.3 to $25.2. Literature agrees that bubbles saturate with CO2 over tall liquid columns and that CO2 stripping is set by gas flow and residence time, not kLa: Sieblist 2011 [repo, abstract], Xing 2009 and 2016 [repo, abstract/snippet], Nickel 2024 review [repo, full text]. Industrial responses so far are internals (a second sparger to compartmentalise a tall tank, patent 12371651 [snippet] https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12371651) and cross-flow spargers (Thermo Fisher note [snippet] https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/BPD/Application-Notes/efficient-operation-evaluation-5-1-sub-working-volumes-app-note.pdf), not wide shallow vessels.
- Wave/rocking and orbital-shaken bags are shallow by nature and used for CHO perfusion (Clincke 2013 is in your back-test), but capped near 1-2 m3 working volume. I found no cultivated-meat tray or thin-film reactor with CO2 data; Mission Barns is said to use an adherent "in-body-like" reactor with no CO2 detail [snippet] https://greenqueen.com.hk/?p=74527.
- Laptop value: the analytic version (cost vs aspect ratio including footprint, building and mixing time) is a day's work and probably worth doing. CFD on a laptop adds little because the limit is a gas-side balance, not a flow pattern.
- Related: Li et al. 2020 designed a 300 m3, 13.75 m tall airlift with CFD and claimed 2e8 cells/mL on microcarriers [Negulescu text, read; GFI page summary] https://gfi.org/deep-dive-cultivated-meat-bioprocess-design/. Neither source says it checked CO2.

### B. Bubble-free gas exchange through an external hollow-fibre loop, with alkaline/CA sweep
- **Status: PARTLY at small scale; OPEN as a quantified design for 20 m3.**
- Membrane aeration is old: silicone tubing, PTFE/hollow fibre, hydrophobic porous membranes. A 1993 comparison found fouling and low growth with silicone tubing, and bubble oxygenation with Pluronic worked well [snippet] https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/bit.260410702. Dynamic membrane aeration (silicone on a rotor) was tested to 200 L in 2009 [snippet] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2677146; a vendor sells 50 and 200 L units and projects 1,000 L [vendor claim] https://www.reprocell.com/product-catalog/featured/biothrust-bioreactor-systems. Silicone-tubing O2 supply fell off above ~14% packed cell volume [snippet, same search]. A patent family claims PDMS hollow-fibre modules replacing the sparger at >=120e6 cells/mL CHO perfusion with separate O2 and air/CO2 paths; applicant's claim, unverified [snippet] https://patents.google.com/patent/US12415980. A 2026 3D-printed membrane module paper targets O2 at bench scale, with no CO2 [snippet] https://www.maxapress.com/data/article/els/preview/pdf/10.1002-elsc.70073.pdf. Repo notes found no kLa or flux numbers for membrane CO2 removal and only bench-scale silicone hollow-fibre CO2 removal in a patent snippet [repo].
- The CO2 side is mature in a different field: extracorporeal CO2 removal (ECCO2R). Carbonic-anhydrase-coated hollow fibres raised CO2 removal 36% (108 mL/min/m2 in a 0.0084 m2 loop) [snippet] https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi.dedup.....91d44783fe4b75b2a8d2887be1018817; acidic sweep gas plus CA is synergistic [snippet, title only]; clinical device flux is limited by bicarbonate-to-CO2 conversion and blood flow, not membrane area [snippet] https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/s40635-020-00301-7. This is the closest thing to "alkaline or CA sweep" and it is not in the cultivated-meat literature I found. Note ECCO2R is about acidic or CA-enhanced conversion on the liquid side; an alkaline sweep in the gas/liquid on the permeate side would pull CO2 as carbonate, which I did not find analysed for cell culture.
- **[INF] Sizing, to show why this is worth a study.** At the floor (120 g/L, 0.149 mmol O2/g/h, respiratory quotient 1.0-1.2) CO2 production is 18-21 mmol/L/h, about 360-430 mol/h per 20 m3. At ECCO2R-like flux (~290 mmol/h/m2 from 108 mL/min/m2 at STP) that is ~1,200-1,500 m2 of membrane, ~60-75 m2/m3. At a ten-times-lower flux it is still only ~600-750 m2/m3 of fibre, which at industrial module prices is plausibly small against $810k/m3 plant cost. The numbers above are order-of-magnitude and use blood data; the real question is whether 120 g/L of cells (packed-volume fraction, viscosity, fouling, sterilisation) can be pumped through fibres at all. Retention may be solved at the same time if the loop is run as a filter, which also links to bottleneck (g).
- Laptop version: adds something, because no one has published the area, pressure-drop, shear and cost balance for 20 m3 at cultivated-meat densities.

### C. Optimal control of perfusion or fill-and-draw
- **Status: DONE in biopharma; marginal for your question.** Model-predictive and economic control exist: hybrid kinetic-stoichiometric MPC (Imperial) [snippet] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10371800/; hybrid-model optimisation of a media-exchange schedule with +50% volumetric productivity [snippet] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11831417/; Sartorius NMPC that switches between stable and economic objectives [snippet] https://www.genengnews.com/topics/bioprocessing/multi-controller-system-major-boost-to-continuous-cho-perfusion/; grey-box design minimising medium and perfusion rate at a target density [snippet] https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2022.948905/pdf; perfusion profile optimisation in pre-stages [snippet, same list]. I found no cultivated-meat dynamic optimisation; GFI's Nov 2025 report on growth models is the closest and I did not read it [snippet] https://gfi.org/cm-tea-report.
- **[INF]** At steady state perfusion is already at its cap, and your fill-and-draw formula already gives the average. Dynamic control can only gain in ramp-up, start-up and recovery, and in bleed timing, a few percent of cost. Biopharma gains reported are mostly in protein titre, not in cost per kg of biomass.

### D. Thermodynamic bounds (Heijnen / von Stockar / Roels)
- **Status: theory DONE for microbes; sparsely applied to mammalian cells; low decision value.** von Stockar's reviews and Heijnen's Gibbs-dissipation correlations exist [snippet] https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/172092/files/jnetdy.2010.024.pdf ; I found no application to CHO or meat cell lines. Calorimetry for mammalian cells gives the oxycaloric equivalent ~470 kJ/mol O2 [snippet] https://mitofit.org/index.php/Gnaiger_1990_BBA and ~467 kJ per mol glucose [snippet] https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00969. Your model already uses 483 kJ/mol O2 (Humbird). A hybridoma study put maintenance at ~60% of energy use at 0.66/day [snippet] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18587939.
- **[INF]** The bound is far below observed cost because maintenance and kinetics, not thermodynamics, bind; you already have a "rough lower bound" row ($3.1/kg). A tighter Gibbs bound would not change what anyone builds or funds.

### E. Computational design of heat-stable growth-factor substitutes
- **Status: PARTLY done, heavily contested by funded groups, and not laptop-compatible.** Computer-assisted FGF2-G3 (nine mutations, Tm +~19 C; half-life <10 h to >7 days claimed) is published (Dvorak 2018) [abstract/snippet] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29278409/ and sold by Qkine [vendor] https://www.reprocell.com/product-catalog/qkine-growth-factors-and-cytokines/recombinant-fgf2-g3-protein-qk053. De novo designed FGFR c-isoform agonists exist from the Baker lab (Cell 2024) [snippet] https://www.bakerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Cell_13439_Modulation_of_FGF_pathway_signalling_2024.pdf. A chemically synthesised bFGF replacement (TCB-32) was announced [press] https://vegconomist.com/manufacturing-technology/the-cultivated-b-unveils-synthetic-growth-factor-to-reduce-cultivated-meat-costs/; FGF2-G3 expressed in Lactococcus lactis (~2 mg/L) [snippet] https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2025.1560426/full; bovine cells engineered to make their own factors [press] https://foodnavigator.com/Article/2024/01/31/cultivated-meat-cells-engineered-to-produce-growth-factors. I found no designed insulin or IGF mimetic for culture.
- Laptop value: low. Design needs GPU structure prediction, and a design is worth nothing without expression and bioassay in a wet lab. Your own tornado puts growth factors at ~$4/kg of $11.5, but several groups are working on it.

### F. Intracellular-pH kinetic model under high CO2
- **Status: PARTLY.** General pHi models exist (Bouret/Counillon 2014 [snippet] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3894979) and a CHO pHi model by Wu, Ray and Shuler 1993 with Na/H antiport, lactate and ammonia [snippet] https://zendy.io/title/10.1021/bp00022a004; I could not confirm it treats CO2. Experimental work (hybridoma, CHO; Zhu 2005, deZengotita 2002, Zanghi 1999) found CO2 effects at constant osmolality are modest (9% growth loss from 67 to 200 mbar in CHO) and that HCO3-/osmolality and pHi drop of ~0.2 units explain much of the inhibition [snippets] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3449846, https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/bit.10176; amino acids such as glycine betaine only partly protect [snippet, same search]. Nothing recent in perfusion at 100+ g/L.
- **[INF]** Many free parameters, no data a laptop can fit them to, and the model's own finding is that engineering CO2 removal beats raising tolerance. A kinetic model could not replace the adaptation experiment it proposes.

### G. Contamination as dynamics
- **Status: PARTLY.** Your model already has loss-per-day and restart time and finds a break-even of ~11 days. Literature: no stochastic contaminant-vs-culture model found for mammalian or cultivated-meat culture. Mycoplasma spikes in a rocking perfusion bioreactor showed effects depending on density and perfusion rate, with 28-day culture assays too slow for decisions and process parameters (DO, ammonia) giving earlier signals [snippet] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6900124. GFI says sterility cost has not been studied for cultivated-meat cells at scale [snippet] https://gfi.org/solutions/cost-sterility-controls-cultivated-meat. Negulescu sized media sterilisation for "1 contamination per 50 years" [read]. Biopharma has modelling of introduction time [snippet] https://www.biopharminternational.com/view/investigating-bioreactor-contamination-modeling-contaminant-introduction-and-ensuring-bioburden-test-reliability.
- Laptop value: the dynamics (bacteria double in 20-60 min vs 24 h for cells; nutrient competition; detection thresholds) are easy to model, and robust conclusions follow in a day: a contaminant in a nutrient-rich medium overtakes cells in hours, so loss rates are set by ingress rate and detection, not growth. What is not available is real contamination-rate data from food-grade tanks; without it the output is a threshold, not a forecast.

### H. Flux-balance analysis for minimum waste and oxygen per kg
- **Status: DONE in several forms; laptop adds little unless tied to your cost model.** CHO genome-scale models and enzyme-constrained FBA exist, including Hill-type lactate/ammonia inhibition [snippet] https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/predictive-modelling-of-cho-cell-culture-using-enzyme-constrained/, asparagine-driven feed design [snippet] https://oar.a-star.edu.sg/storage/2/2rnn56jnx8/genome-scale-modeling-of-cho-cells-unravel-the-critical-role-of-asparagine.pdf, steady-state analysis of continuous cultures [snippet] https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.09708; bovine satellite-cell GEM BtaSBML2986 [snippet] https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.09.627468.full.pdf; a proteome-constrained pig myoblast model with lactate/ammonium stress [snippet] https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:65b6509d-213c-42f8-8ea6-9e511456fb2c/files/rz029p711x; a GFI grant on a computational bovine media model [snippet] https://gfi.org/grants/optimizing-growth-media-bovine-cells/. I found no chicken GEM. Empirically, an alpha-ketoglutarate medium cut ammonia production >5-fold in adipose progenitors (Hubalek 2023) [snippet via Pakbin search] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12364936/.
- **[INF]** "Minimum waste" from FBA is a stoichiometric bound that cells do not follow (Warburg-type overflow is regulatory), so the minimum would mislead unless constrained by measured uptake rates.

### I. Better gravity settler at 20 m3
- **Status: PARTLY; the physics is established, the large-scale design is not published.** Inclined/lamellar settlers: Searles 1994, Batt 1990 [snippet] ; Romann 2023 (settler for a 2,000 L CHO process, 94% separation above design flow, 1.46 h residence) [repo, full text]; Coronel 2020 (avian cells to 50e6/mL, doubling time slowed with the settler) [repo, full text]; acoustic settlers cap near 200-1,000 L/day [repo]. Your own notes already derive ~0.7-1.0 cm/h design overflow velocity and ~83 m2 for a 20 m3 tank at one volume per day [repo, inference]. I found no cultivated-meat settler paper at 3,000 L (the figure in your brief) [snippet search] and no data on settling of clumps of mixed size at 100+ g/L (hindered settling, i.e., Richardson-Zaki-type slowing at high solids, which at 20-40% packed fraction cuts velocity severely) **[INF]**.
- Laptop value: a settler model with hindered settling and an oxygen-during-stay balance is feasible, but the cells' anoxia tolerance (your note suggests 1-1.5 h survived by CHO) and clump behaviour are exactly the unmeasured quantities.

### J. Bottom-up plant cost model with switchable sterility items
- **Status: PARTLY; the itemised data exist, the "what does a cheap plant leave out" analysis does not.** Humbird's cost tables are itemised [repo, read]; CE Delft and Negulescu assume food-grade rather than sterile pharma plants [repo, read]; GFI states that sterility controls drive cost [snippet] https://gfi.org/solutions/cost-sterility-controls-cultivated-meat; Rethink Priorities compared TEAs [snippet] https://rethinkpriorities.org/publications/cultured-meat-a-comparison-of-techno-economic-analyses; the Unjournal workshop has an open Monte Carlo cost model [snippet] https://unjournal.github.io/cm_pq_modeling/learn.html. Your own `plant_capital_crosscheck.md` already contains most of the comparison: Humbird $683k/m3 total vs $167-277k for CE Delft/Negulescu, which differ in clean rooms and Lang factor (~7.2 vs ~3).
- Laptop value: real, because bottleneck (e) is where the model sees the largest step (5x). Risk: without equipment quotes the result is a re-labelling of Humbird's line items.

### K. Check Negulescu's 262 m3 airlift ($28k/m3) against the CO2 balance
- **Status: OPEN, and quick. [read] (Negulescu text).** The paper checked oxygen: OTR 13.5 mmol/L/h for the airlift vs OUR ~10.3 at 100 g FW/L (so it ties to your density limits), set the O2:CO2 ratio at 1.2, and attributes the lower COGS vs Humbird to assuming 100 g FW/L, noting Humbird's CO2 inhibition at large scale. I did not find any CO2 stripping check for the airlift; the capex of the airlift is priced from a 1997 Sulzer figure scaled by a 0.6 power [repo].
- **[INF] First-pass balance:** CO2 production at 100 g FW/L ~12.4 mmol/L/h (10.3 x 1.2). A 13.75 m column with gas saturating at 100 mbar (3.9 mmol CO2 per litre gas) needs ~3.2 L gas/L/h, a superficial velocity of ~12 mm/s; at 50 mbar, ~24 mm/s. That is not obviously impossible in an airlift riser, but is above your 3-6 mm/s guidance and ignores foam, microcarrier damage and hydrostatic effects. The design neither passes nor fails; it depends on tolerable CO2. A day's check, not a project.

### L. More demand data (price premiums and shares of premium products)
- **Status: PARTLY.** I found no meta-analysis of willingness to pay or price elasticity for cultivated meat; a 48-study meta-analysis covers acceptance predictors and treats price as secondary [snippet] https://vegconomist.com/studies-numbers/meta-analysis-reveals-key-factors-determine-willingness-consume-cultivated-meat/. Choice experiments range from discounts demanded to >2x premium depending on framing [snippets] https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2021.594650. Your six-product table is, as far as I found, original.
- Laptop value: moderate; data are public tables, not microdata. The risk is that it refines a fit to a narrow set of "virtue premium" products that differ from cultivated meat (not an ordinary substitute at parity).

### T. Meta-analysis of published perfusion runs (minimum cell-specific perfusion rate)
- **Status: PARTLY; scattered numbers, no cultivated-meat or cross-process synthesis found.** Reported CSPR minima span ~8-10 pL/cell/day in an optimised medium (your note, a 500 mL Wave run) [repo], 10-30 pL/c/d in "novel media blends" to 200e6 cells/mL [snippet] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12314661/, ~50-113 pL/c/d in N-1 perfusion [snippet] https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2025.1608889/pdf, ~100 pL/c/d with a push-to-low method for another line [snippet, same search]. Method matters (push-to-low, perfusion without bleeding, semi-perfusion give different minima), and CSPR is clone- and medium-specific [snippet] https://qa1-assets.thermofisher.cn/TFS-Assets/BPD/Reference-Materials/perfusion-terminology-white-paper.pdf. The Bielser 2018 critical review of perfusion and the Wolf/Bielser/Morbidelli book discuss "push-to-low" and a 0.05 nL/cell/day target [snippet] https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4VE2drX7. None expresses CSPR per gram or ties it to ammonia, lactate and cell size, which is what your model needs.
- Laptop value: real and concrete. Caution below on selection bias.

### M-S (brief; not requested in depth)
- **M (value-of-information of lab measurements):** model tool only; your tornado is already a first pass. Prior art in cultivated meat: none found.
- **N (fat as 5-20% ingredient):** commercial prior art exists, e.g., Mission Barns approved cultivated pork fat blended into plant-based products, with its own undisclosed TEA [snippet] https://agfundernews.com/mission-barns-secures-usda-green-light-for-cultivated-fat-plans-q3-launches. Your model could add the per-kg numbers; the open question is fat-cell lines and structuring, not blending arithmetic.
- **O (seafood animals spared), P (build-out size), Q (policy instruments), R (learning curve):** all are cheap extensions of the existing model. I did not search prior art for O, Q, R; for P, the GFI and Humbird plant tables provide inputs [repo].
- **S (claims checker):** your back-test already is the prototype and found SuperMeat's stated output 2.4x its own density and growth, and Pasitka's density 6x its cell count [repo, read]. GFI's Nov 2025 report on cell-mass modelling argues most TEAs overestimate cell mass up to ~3x [snippet] https://proteinproductiontechnology.com/post/new-techno-economic-analysis-finds-cell-density-and-accurate-biomass-modeling-key-to-cutting-cultivated-meat-costs. A published checklist for cost claims exists in your METHODS.md. A public tool is OPEN as far as I found.

## 3. Value: who would act on the result

Ranked by how much the result could change a decision by a lab, company or funder; the model's rough judgement, made while building the model with an AI assistant **[INF]**.

1. **B (+K, A as sub-cases).** If required membrane area and cost per m3 come out small, a bioprocess engineer or funder has a concrete component to test (a membrane loop at 20-200 L with CHO or chicken cells). If they come out large or the loop cannot carry dense cells, it closes a hope cheaply. It is on the model's largest cost lever (CO2/gas).
2. **J (plant-cost itemisation).** Funders choosing between "sterile plant" and "food-grade plant" research; companies like Vow or Believer building; TEA authors. The model's own result says this is the step from ~$11.5 to ~$6.6/kg.
3. **T (CSPR meta-analysis).** Media developers and TEA authors; sets the litres per kg (the back-test found real runs used 1.2-2.8x the model's medium). It also informs how much a cheaper-medium effort is worth.
4. **S (claims checker), as a public tool plus ledger.** Funders, journalists and TEA authors; deters inconsistent claims. High credibility value, limited effect on engineering.
5. **G (contamination thresholds).** Companies and funders considering cheap plants; useful only with measured loss rates, which would need food-grade tank data from companies.
6. **I (settler).** Retention at 20 m3 (bottleneck g) is the model's hoped-for device; a credible settler sizing would help a company decide whether to try gravity settling or filters. Needs lab data to be decisive.
7. **K.** Cheap, but a TEA authors' correction note, not a project.
8. **H, A, L, N, M, P, Q, R, O.** Moderate or marginal; P/Q/R help communication and advocacy, not engineering.
9. **C, D, E, F.** Lowest decision value for the reasons above.

## 4. Missing projects that I think beat the weakest listed ones

Each is tied to a bottleneck; prior art noted.

**M1. Medium regeneration and in-loop ammonia removal in the model's perfusion economics (bottleneck c).** Medium is 55-95% of cost in several estimates [snippet] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12364936/. Alkaline stripping removed >82% of ammonia from spent medium at pH 12 in 15 min with glucose largely intact, and a 50:50 blend of treated and fresh medium supported lamb satellite cells (Pakbin 2025) [snippet] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12364936/; membranes, Chlorella and alpha-ketoglutarate feeds are other routes [snippets] https://journal.hep.com.cn/smab/EN/1160585298243543984. The authors themselves say no proper techno-economic analysis exists. What your model adds: when the minimum CSPR is set by waste (ammonia, lactate) rather than nutrient depletion, removing waste in the loop could cut litres per kg more than medium price does, and it ties to the 1.2-2.8x medium gap in the back-test. Laptop-feasible and genuinely open. Failure mode: nutrients and growth factors lost in treatment; added sterility steps.

**M2. A reported-run ledger: merge T and S.** Collect every public cultivated-meat and relevant CHO run (density, doubling time, cell mass, medium flow, vessel, depth, gas flow, output) from papers, regulatory dossiers and company reports, with a mass-balance consistency flag. Prior art: your back-test, GFI cell-mass report [snippet] https://gfi.org/cm-tea-report, the TEA comparison by Rethink Priorities [snippet]. No open ledger found. More durable than either T or S alone, and the data feed the model's calibration.

**M3. The gas-flow ceiling: what limits superficial gas velocity in a 20 m3 animal-cell tank (bottleneck a).** Your tornado shows gas flow as the largest single risk (28.0 to 11.5 $/kg from 2 to 6 mm/s), and the floor assumes the top of the guidance. A literature-and-physics study would collect what actually caps it: foam, bubble-disengagement cell damage and Pluronic protection (the 1993 comparison found Pluronic enabled direct bubble oxygenation [snippet]), evaporation and humidification at high flow, antifoam fouling of filters, compressor energy and off-gas handling. Prior art: scattered biopharma knowledge (Sieblist, Xing, Nienow); I found no cultivated-meat consolidation. Laptop-feasible; reduces the biggest uncertainty on the floor. Failure mode: it is a literature synthesis, so results are bounded by unpublished industrial practice.

**M4. Cell-line target product profile (inverse model) (bottlenecks a-d).** Invert your model: given a plant class and a target cost, what density, doubling time, cell mass, waste per gram, and CO2 tolerance must a line deliver, and which are interchangeable? Users: cell-line developers and GFI-type funders writing calls. Prior art: none found as a quantified spec; GFI grants fund lines without published specs [snippet] https://gfi.org/grants/optimizing-growth-media-bovine-cells/. Failure: spec is only as good as the model.

**M5. Seafood-cell CO2 tolerance: synthesis and experiment design (bottleneck a, and demand).** RESULTS.md says the seafood fillet share falls from 45% to 16% if fish cells tolerate 50 not 100 mbar, and "nobody has measured". Your repo has `fish_cell_co2_ph.md`; I did not re-search it. A short synthesis plus a minimal-lab protocol for a 1-2 week lab test is cheap and could unlock a grant or a collaborator. Failure: no data to synthesise; may only confirm the gap.

**M6. Seed-train and start-up economics in food-grade tanks (bottlenecks b, e).** A 24-h-doubling line needs weeks to scale from a vial to 20 m3 and every step is a contamination exposure; cost of inoculum per kg and the share of the year in seed trains for perfusion vs fill-and-draw. Prior art: Humbird includes a seed train [repo]; I found no focused study. Overlaps with J; treat as a J module.

**M7. Heat removal at 20-200 m3 (cheap check).** **[INF]** At 120 g/L, 0.149 mmol O2/g/h and 470 kJ/mol, heat is ~2.3 kW/m3, ~47 kW per 20 m3 and ~470 kW per 200 m3; with jacket area per volume falling with scale and a cooling-water margin of only a few kelvin (cells die above ~39 C), it may bind before CO2 in very large tanks. Search found no quantitative jacket figures and one source saying mammalian culture "does not generate significant heat" [snippet] https://bdo.com/insights/industries/life-sciences/principles-of-mammalian-cell-culture-process-scale-up, which is a low-density view. A one-week addition to the model's list of limits. Failure: might simply not bind at 20 m3.

**M8. Hybrid-product economics beyond fat (demand/entry).** Not just fat: cultivated cells as 5-20% of blends and flavour/fat enhancers, with the model's cost floor and price sensitivity. Prior art: Mission Barns fat approval [snippet], GFI TEA statements on hybrid products [snippet] https://gfi.org/blog/cultivated-meat-lca-tea/. Overlaps with N; pick only if demand work is wanted.

I rank M1, M2, M3 above C, D, E, F and above most of the "easy extension" group (O-R) for decision value.

## 5. My ranking

**Best six overall**
1. **B, with K and the analytic part of A folded in: the gas-exchange design space at 20 m3.** Largest lever (CO2 removal sets density), thin prior art at scale, concrete test it hands to an experimenter. Treat the output as "required flux and area" not a reactor design.
2. **M1: medium regeneration and in-loop ammonia removal in the model.** Genuinely open (the authors of the leading paper say a TEA is missing), tied to the 1.2-2.8x medium gap, and changes what the minimum perfusion rate means.
3. **M2 (T + S): the reported-run ledger and minimum-perfusion meta-analysis.** The most robust deliverable; improves every other number and gives funders a tool; prior art is scattered, not synthesised.
4. **J: bottom-up plant cost with switchable sterility items.** The 5x plant-cost gap is the model's biggest unknown; the work is feasible on public line items, though the risk of false precision is real.
5. **M3: the gas-flow ceiling.** The tornado says this is the largest single downside on the floor; a literature-and-physics study can narrow it quickly.
6. **G: contamination thresholds,** scoped to the question "what loss rate and detection delay can a food-grade plant tolerate", feeding J. Weak on data, but it supports or breaks the case for a cheaper plant.

**Three to drop**
- **D (thermodynamic bounds):** the bound is not binding and is already approximated; no user changes behaviour.
- **E (de novo growth-factor substitutes):** not a laptop project, already crowded (FGF2-G3, TCB-32, endogenous expression, Baker-lab FGFR agonists), and not one of your bottlenecks a-g.
- **C (optimal control):** large prior art in biopharma and a small expected gain over steady-state perfusion.

F and H are next closest to dropping: F has too many free parameters, H is largely done and its minimum is not what cells do.
