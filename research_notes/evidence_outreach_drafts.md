**Suggested recipients:** contract bioprocess labs or academic groups with perfusion rigs and meat-relevant cells (for
example university cellular-agriculture centres with public collaboration contacts; GFI's research-grant team, to ask about
funding; a cell-line provider whose lines are publicly available, such as the Tufts TU-GFI lines, for permission to use them).

**Subject:** Quote request: two bench studies (ammonium tolerance at high density; hollow-fibre life) with a meat cell line

> Dear [NAME / team],
>
> I would like a quote for two small studies whose results would be published openly and would replace the two most
> uncertain inputs in an open cost model of cultivated meat.
>
> **Study 1: ammonium tolerance at density.** A meat-relevant cell line [line, e.g. a publicly available bovine or chicken
> line] in a 1–2 L perfusion at 10–20% cells by volume, serum-free, pH fixed. Ammonium (as NH4Cl, with total ammonium
> measured daily) held at 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7 mM for 10 days each; then 4 and 7 mM repeated on cells raised slowly to that
> level. Readouts: specific growth rate, viability, glucose, lactate and glutamine uptake. Answer sought: the highest level
> that keeps growth within 10% of control. Our rough cost estimate: $15–40k over two to three months.
>
> **Study 2: filter life with meat cells.** The same culture at 15–20% cells by volume with 0.025 m² hollow-fibre modules:
> one 0.2 µm PES or PVDF, one wide-pore (e.g. 2–7 µm ceramic or polymer), and ideally one low-cost dialyser-type fibre; flux
> 2.5 and 5 L/m²/h; with [ANTIFOAM, DOSE] and poloxamer 188 at 2 g/L; one module backflushed daily. Record transmembrane
> pressure and sieving (protein and a small solute) against litres per m² and against days, to failure. Our rough cost
> estimate: $20–50k over about six weeks.
>
> Could you say whether you could run either study, at what cost and timeline, and what you would need from us (cell line,
> medium, consumables)?
>
