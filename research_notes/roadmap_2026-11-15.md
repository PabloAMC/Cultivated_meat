## 2. Frontier projects

Grouped by what they derisk. Each says which number in the model it could move, how, and how it fails.

### Track A — A virtual 20 m³ tank (CFD with FluidX3D)

**Why now, and why the earlier "CFD adds little" no longer holds.** The 2026-11-14 review was right that the **CO₂ cap**
is a gas-side balance a CFD run cannot improve. But the scenarios since then lean on three things that *are* flow
problems and are currently set by rules of thumb:

- the **mixing cap** uses Humbird's (H/T)^2.5 scaling; at aspect 1.0 it jumps from 189 to 551 g/L, and the best and
  central scenarios use tanks 0.75 and 0.5 times as deep as wide — outside where that correlation was fitted;
- the **alkaline return from the CO₂ column**: the pH-exposure test ("rise × share" vs Paul et al. 2020) is my inference
  from one study, and it depends on how fast the returned stream is diluted;
- **gradients at 120–258 g/L**: oxygen uptake of 18–40 mmol/L/h is microbial-fermenter territory, where large-scale
  gradients are known to matter (Haringa et al.; Brorens, Ottens & Haringa 2026 is already in our sources).

**What FluidX3D can and cannot do** (from its README): GPU lattice Boltzmann, single phase or free surface, Smagorinsky
LES, moving/rotating STL geometry (impellers), a temperature extension (a D3Q7 advection–diffusion field we can use as a
passive tracer), passive or two-way particles (Lagrangian "cells"), force/torque on objects (power number). It cannot
do gas and liquid together (no bubbles, no foam), has no chemistry (no sink term without editing the source), constant
viscosity only. **Licence: free for non-commercial research; results from modified source require publishing the
modified source; no commercial use** — fine for a paper or the EA Forum, not for a startup.

**Does it fit the laptop?** Apple M4, 16 GB unified, ~120 GB/s. At 55 B/cell, ~150–190 M cells usable. A 20 m³ tank at
6–7 mm spacing is ~70–150 M cells. By scaling the README's M2 Pro figure (200 GB/s → ~2,400 MLUPs FP16), the M4 gives
roughly 1,400 MLUPs; 60 s of tank time at tip speed ~1.5 m/s is ~2–4 h of compute. **Feasible.** Caveat: the Kolmogorov
scale at 40 W/m³ is ~70 µm, so this is LES, not DNS; peak dissipation near the impeller is a sub-grid-model output, so
the shear sub-project must be validated. A rented A100 (~10 GLUPs, a few $/h) gives 5–7× finer grids if needed.
Note: Apple has deprecated OpenCL but it still runs; check this first (half a day).

Sub-projects, in order:

| # | question | method | model number it moves | P(moves a number by >10%) | effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A0 | Does the code reproduce known tanks? | Rushton and pitched-blade in a lab tank: power number (torque), mixing time vs the Grenville/Nienow correlation | none; gate for the rest | — | 1–2 wk |
| A1 | Mixing time vs aspect ratio (0.5–1.6) at fixed W/m³, one vs two impellers, 20 m³ | tracer pulse, 95% homogeneity | `mixing_time_ratio`, the mixing cap in squat tanks | 50% | 1–2 wk |
| A2 | How fast is the column's alkaline return diluted? | tracer plume + Lagrangian cells; exposure = ∫ΔpH dt per cell; vary return point | the column's safe pass rate (11 vs 22–85 per hour) and so $7.5 vs $8.6 | 55% | 1–2 wk |
| A3 | Oxygen/CO₂/pH gradients at 120–258 g/L, 20 and 200 m³ | edit the temperature extension into a scalar with a uniform sink and a sparger-zone source; cell lifelines | whether 200 m³ is ever worth it; the tank-size lever | 35% | 3–4 wk |
| A5 | Oxygen transfer per watt for impellers and spargers, now that oxygen limits Central (Pablo, 2026-11-16) | FluidX3D gives each impeller's power number, pumping and flow pattern (single phase); kLa itself needs bubbles, so pair it with published kLa correlations per impeller type (van't Riet style) or an Euler–Euler run in OpenFOAM | the vessel's oxygen multiplier, and with it Central's oxygen limit (177 g/L; worth up to $1.8/kg if antifoam's cut were offset) | 40% | 2–3 wk |
| A4 | Peak shear/dissipation for large impellers in squat tanks vs cell thresholds (Godoy-Silva) | LES dissipation field, validated against PIV data | none directly; a "go/no-go" on the squat-tank designs | 20% | 2 wk |

Not FluidX3D: **bubble bursting and foam** (the gas-flow cap's real unknowns) need a two-phase VOF code such as Basilisk
(open source; the method of Walls et al. 2017, already in our sources). Walls et al. and the Wageningen papers in
`../articles/` have largely done the single-bubble physics, so I would not start there. Foam cannot be simulated
reliably at all (`gas_exchange_study.md` §8.3).

**Track verdict:** worth doing as A0 → A1 → A2 (5–6 weeks). It turns two rules of thumb the scenarios rest on into
checked numbers, and it is a good public artefact (renders of a 20 m³ cultivated-meat tank are rare). Probability the
whole track changes a headline scenario by more than $1/kg: ~35%.

### Track B — Inverse model: a target product profile for cell lines (M4)

Invert `floor_physics.explore`: for a plant class and a target cost ($7 parity, $10, $15), give the set of cell
properties that reach it — ammonia and lactate per kg, doubling time, wet mass per cell, pCO₂ tolerance, growth-factor
need — and which trade against which (a Pareto surface). Users: cell-line developers and funders writing calls.
Every switch already exists, so this is mostly search and presentation. **Not found in the literature as a quantified
spec.** 1–2 weeks. Failure mode: the spec is only as good as the model, so it must be shown with the scenario bands.
**My top recommendation for value per hour.**

### Track C — Value of information and an experiment portfolio

The gas-exchange note already gives, for each open cell question, a cost if it resolves well, a cost if badly, a
probability, and a price for the deciding experiment ($100–300k, 2–3 months). Do this formally across all switches:
expected $/kg reduction (and expected share gain) per experiment dollar, with correlations. Output: a ranked list of
5–10 experiments with protocols, the thing a GFI or EU Horizon/EIC call could fund. 2–3 weeks. Builds on B. Strong fit
with your forecasting habit; also keeps the model honest about which probabilities are guesses.

### Track D — Reported-run ledger (M2 = T + S)

An open table of every public cultivated-meat and relevant CHO run: density, doubling time, cell mass, medium per kg,
vessel, depth, gas flow, output, with a mass-balance flag. The back-test already found SuperMeat's output 2.4× its own
density × growth and Pasitka's density 6× its cell count. Durable, citable, and it recalibrates every other number.
3–5 weeks; can be split with subagents per source class (papers / regulatory dossiers — FDA, FSANZ A1269, EFSA — /
company reports). Failure mode: selection bias; most runs do not report cell mass.

### Track E — Where today's cost already works: hybrids and fat

Not in the model. At 10% inclusion in a plant-based product, $26.6/kg cells adds ~$2.7/kg to the blend; the demand
question is how much "real meat" authenticity and flavour 5–20% buys. This may be the only place the central scenario
reaches parity, and it is where companies are going (Mission Barns fat). Extends `foothold.py` and the four-product
logit with an inclusion-rate dimension. 2–3 weeks. Highest relevance for animal welfare in the next decade; also the
honest counterweight to the main result (0.3% of world meat in the central case).

### Track F — Cost-optimal growth rate (the proposal)

`growth_rate.py` is an envelope. The proposal's full version (a coarse proteome-allocation model, competing overflow
theories, calibrated on CHO chemostat series, coupled to `explore`) is 6–8 weeks and needs a lab partner for its
prediction. The scenarios now treat doubling time as an explicit switch worth only ~$1.7/kg (36 h → 24 h), which lowers
the value of this project: it matters mainly if slower growth cuts waste a lot (the "threshold" overflow case). Do a
two-day check first: in the envelope, how large must the waste reduction at 48 h be to beat the capital penalty? If
it needs more than the 5-fold the CHO data hint at, drop it.

### Track G — Medium regeneration TEA (M1, remaining half)

Price ammonia stripping, zeolite/ion exchange and dialysis per litre regenerated, including growth-factor loss, and add
a "regenerated fraction" switch. Medium is the second-largest switch and each litre also passes the filter, so this
compounds. The leading paper's authors say no TEA exists. 2–3 weeks.

### Track H — A cheap physical experiment (optional, for fun)

The gas-flow note says foam height against gas velocity in a protein-rich medium "would by itself move my 45% by
15–20 points". A bench bubble column (1–2 m tall acrylic tube, aquarium sparger, mass-flow controller) with BSA or
serum-containing medium, poloxamer at 0/1/2/5 g/L, stepped at 1–9 mm/s, is a few hundred euros and a weekend. Without
real spent medium the result transfers only partly, so it is a probe, not an answer. Better: offer the protocol to a
university lab (Wageningen's bioprocess group wrote the classic sparging papers in `../articles/`).

### Considered and not recommended

- Thermodynamic bounds, de novo growth-factor design, optimal control, intracellular-pH model: as in `project_review.md`.
- Bubble-burst CFD: done in the literature (above).
- A fully resolved DNS of a production tank: impossible at 70 µm Kolmogorov scale on any machine we have.
