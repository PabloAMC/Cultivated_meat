# Tonne prices of the five amino acids hydrolysate cannot supply (2024–2026)

Research date: 2026-11-21. Evidence pass only; no input changed. Question: what glutamine, arginine, asparagine, cystine
and serine cost by the tonne, at feed and food grade, and what that does to the floor (`aa_intensity` 0.232 kg/kg,
`aa_bulk_price` $6.36/kg). Dollars are as reported (2024–2026); at these sizes the step to 2025 dollars (±3%) is below the
spread of the sources and is not applied.

**Strength codes.** **T** = trade statistics (customs-based unit values); **I** = price-reporting index (ChemAnalyst, Expert
Market Research: methods not public, pages often paywalled, read through search summaries); **A** = trade-aggregator
averages over all lot sizes (Pharmaoffer, Tridge; skewed upward by small lots); **L** = marketplace or catalogue listing
(weak: asking prices, often undated, sometimes placeholders); **G** = GFI Dec 2025 report (interviews and quotes).

GFI's own caveat matters here: its feed-grade column is "a hypothetical low price ... for feed-grade amino acids listed on
aggregate supplier sites" (Table 1 caption), and for asparagine no feed price was found. Its real quotes are the two food
columns. For serine the gap is 3× (10 vs 30), and GFI's Table 2 puts world serine production at about **400 t a year** against
**7,416 t** (FSF4, most efficient) to 24,019 t that a 250 kt/yr industry would need. Tyrosine (200 t) and proline (500 t) are
in the same position. Arginine (60 kt) and glutamine (8 kt) already have tonne markets.

**Not found:** any published tonne quote for asparagine, cystine or serine; any feed-grade glutamine series; producer
announcements of serine capacity with a price. UN Comtrade's HS codes do not isolate these five: glutamine and asparagine sit
in 2924.19 (other acyclic amides, China exports 2025: $915 M, 440 kt, $2.08/kg), serine in 2922.50 (dominated by threonine,
$1.79/kg in 2024), cystine in 2930.90 ($3.35/kg). Only 2925.29 (arginine with other imines, metformin among them) is close.
Chinese 10-digit codes would isolate them; I could not query China Customs from here.

## What it does to the floor

Weights: GFI Table 2, minimum-estimate tonnes (FSF4) and maximum (modified CHO). The five are 51.1% of FSF4's amino acids by
mass and 49.8% of the CHO profile's (FSF4 uses cysteine, not cystine, so cystine has zero weight there). The other 16 amino
acids are held at GFI's feed price. Amino acids a kilo of cells = weighted price × 0.232.

| prices for the five | FSF4-weighted $/kg | $/kg cells | change vs model's $1.48 | CHO-weighted $/kg | $/kg cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GFI feed (model's basis) | 5.74 | 1.33 | −0.15 | 6.47 | 1.50 |
| this pass, low (Arg 2.9, Asn 10, Cys2 8, Gln 5, Ser 10) | 5.58 | 1.29 | −0.19 | 6.12 | 1.42 |
| **this pass, central (Arg 3.5, Asn 15, Cys2 12, Gln 7, Ser 20)** | **7.90** | **1.83** | **+0.35** | **8.24** | **1.91** |
| central with serine at GFI's lowest real quote (30) | 9.60 | 2.23 | +0.75 | 9.38 | 2.18 |
| this pass, high (Arg 5, Asn 30, Cys2 30, Gln 10, Ser 40) | 12.69 | 2.94 | +1.46 | 13.18 | 3.06 |

(The model uses the unweighted mean of 22, $6.36, giving $1.48 a kilo of cells; the floor is $2.00.)

Reading:
- **Arginine and glutamine are where GFI put them** (arginine at its feed price, glutamine near its low food quote).
- **Serine decides it.** Each $10/kg on serine adds about $0.39 a kilo of cells (FSF4 weights). At today's evidence it is
  probably dearer than GFI's feed figure, and the market is so small that an industry buying 7,000+ t would be buying new
  capacity, not today's surplus. A rough judgement (mine, not measured): the chance that serine at 5–10 kt/yr settles at or below
  $10/kg is about 35%; at or below $20, about 65%. Serine is made by fermentation from glucose or enzymatically from glycine
  and methanol, and comparable fermentation amino acids at scale (threonine, lysine, valine) sell at $1–3/kg, so a floor near
  $10 is not implausible in the long run.
- **On today's prices the floor rises by about $0.35–0.45 a kilo of cells** (from $2.00 to about $2.35–2.45), with a range
  of about −$0.2 to +$1.6.

## Recommendation for the parent session (not applied)

1. Keep `aa_bulk_price` as the floor's input, but consider moving its most likely value from $6.36 to about **$7.9**
   (FSF4-weighted, this pass's central prices for the five, GFI feed for the rest), with **lo ≈ $5.6** (this pass's low) and
   **hi ≈ $12.7** (this pass's high; the current hi, $11.59 = GFI's low food-grade mean, sits inside it). Floor: about +$0.35 a kilo.
2. If the floor is meant as a long-run price at industry scale rather than 2024–2026 prices, $6.36 is defensible, but the
   note should then say the serine price assumes new capacity.
3. Either way, add one sentence to the input's note: serine is the largest single cost (30% of FSF4's amino-acid cost) and
   the least evidenced price; a supplier quote ([evidence_outreach_drafts.md](evidence_outreach_drafts.md), draft a) would settle it.
